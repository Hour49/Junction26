# Junction China reopens Beijing's door to Helsinki

*Research date: 2026-11-09. Built from the notes in `research_notes/Junction China hackathon/`.*

Junction China is the official China edition of the Finnish Junction hackathon. Its latest edition, **JUNCTION CHINA 2026, "Hack What's Next"**, ran **6 to 8 Nov 2026** at the ZGC International Technology Trade Center in Haidian, Beijing, and ended the day before this report was written ([hackjunction.cn](https://hackjunction.cn)). It was a free, in-person, 40+ hour "semi-closed" hackathon with three company-set tracks (Xiaomi MiMo for AI, GALBOT for embodied AI and robotics, LLVISION for smart wearables), a **RMB 90,000** prize pool and one prize that matters more than the money: **track winners qualify directly for Junction 2027 in Helsinki**. Beijing's science and technology authorities backed it, and Junction Oy co-organized it, which makes it a government-supported regional flagship rather than a student-run JunctionX spin-off ([Sina Finance / CNS](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-08/doc-inixyrxh7498525.shtml)). Nearly 300 contestants took part, mostly from Chinese universities, with **7 international teams**. China editions have run irregularly: Tsinghua in 2018, Chengdu in 2020, Tianjin in 2021 and Chengdu in 2023, with none in 2025. **No 2027 China edition has been announced**, so Hour 49 has nothing to apply to yet. If one is announced, the team can reach Beijing on Air China's new nonstop flight from Bucharest. The catch is that Romania's 30-day visa-free entry to China is confirmed only until **31 Dec 2026**, so a 2027 trip may need a visa.

## Beijing's 2026 edition paired three tech firms with a Helsinki ticket

The 2026 event ran under a long official Chinese title that places it inside the **2026 Zhongguancun Forum series** as a "China–Europe Youth Innovation Industry Matchmaking Event and Junction China 2026 International Geek Carnival" ([Sina Finance / CNS](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-08/doc-inixyrxh7498525.shtml)). The **Beijing Municipal Science and Technology Commission** and the **Zhongguancun Science Park Administrative Committee** guided it. The organizers were the Beijing International Science and Technology Cooperation Center, **Junction Oy (Finland)**, KR STAR Innovation (氪星创服) and Haidian's Shangdi Subdistrict (same source). At the event, these bodies and the Finnish Aalto innovation association (probably Aaltoes) launched a "Beijing–Finland frontier industry cooperation mechanism" covering talent and two-way overseas expansion. **11 Finnish startups and investors** pitched in a parallel matchmaking session (same source). That shows the event's real purpose: it serves Beijing's industrial diplomacy with Finland as much as it serves the hackers.

The schedule was compact. Check-in was at 13:00 on 6 Nov, an international opening forum started at 14:00, hacking ran from 19:00 that evening to **17:30 on 8 Nov**, and all times were Beijing time (UTC+8) ([hackjunction.cn](https://hackjunction.cn)). The site does not explain "semi-closed". Together with the travel support on offer, it suggests applicants were screened, but the site never says so.

| Track | Challenge partner | Focus |

| Artificial Intelligence | Xiaomi MiMo | LLMs, multimodal AI, AI agents, multi-agent collaboration |
| Embodied AI & Robotics | GALBOT (银河通用) | Humanoid robots, robot learning, dexterous manipulation |
| Smart Wearables | LLVISION (亮亮视野) | AR/VR/MR, 3D vision, digital twins, spatial intelligence |

Source: [hackjunction.cn](https://hackjunction.cn). The site's other partners are OSCHINA, BerryCode, ZION, Unity Developer Community, Tuanjie Codely, **FinnCham China**, Cornea Sense, SILKWAY and TNIH, plus Nordic ecosystem partners **Slush, DASH, maria 01, Aaltoes and Startup Sauna** (same source).

Each track paid **RMB 20,000 / 6,000 / 4,000** for first, second and third place, so a team that wins a track takes home far less than the €100k advertised for Helsinki. Winners also get career and incubation opportunities with the partner companies and **direct qualification for Junction 2027 in Helsinki** ([hackjunction.cn](https://hackjunction.cn)). Press coverage adds joint R&D with the challenge companies ([Sina Finance / CNS](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-08/doc-inixyrxh7498525.shtml)). The site does not say whether the Helsinki qualification includes flights. Participants got free registration, daily essentials, meals and **travel support for eligible participants from outside Beijing, Tianjin and Hebei**, with no amounts or rules given. Accommodation is not mentioned ([hackjunction.cn](https://hackjunction.cn)).

Registration was free, and **each person applied individually** through an external Chinese form service (jsjform), not a Junction domain. Teams could be solo or up to **five people**, declared before the event or formed on site. The call was open to developers, designers, PMs, makers, students and non-technical creatives, with **no published age or nationality rules** ([hackjunction.cn](https://hackjunction.cn)). No deadline, selection criteria or acceptance rate was published. The official contact is **info@hackjunction.cn**, and no WeChat, Xiaohongshu or LinkedIn account for the China edition was found. No winners had been published as of 9 Nov 2026.

## Four earlier China editions left few winners on record

Chinese press called 2026 the first time the Junction brand "landed in China" ([Sina Finance / CNS](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-08/doc-inixyrxh7498525.shtml)). The record shows otherwise, so read that as PR framing. Junction's first China event was **"JUNCTION x Tsinghua", 18 to 20 May 2018** in Beijing. Tsinghua co-hosted it as part of its creative competition and invited Aalto University teams ([Tsinghua News](https://www.tsinghua.edu.cn/info/1175/19247.htm)). That November, a Tsinghua team won first place in OUHUA's building-intelligence challenge at Junction 2018 in Helsinki with "IVision Lamp", a smart lamp using vision and multi-sensor fusion. Tsinghua called it the first Junction challenge win for one of its teams (same source). This is still the best-documented China-linked Junction win.

**JUNCTION X Chengdu 2020** ran 28 to 30 Aug 2020 at the China Western International Expo City, alongside a global 5G industry summit, with track and overall awards on the final day ([Dahe Piaowu](https://www.dahepiao.com/news1/20200820152758.html)). **JunctionX Tianjin** ended on 23 May 2021 with game-development and digital-connectivity tracks. Students from Hebei University of Technology's Finland campus took first in game development and second and third in digital connectivity ([HEBUT FC news](https://finland.hebut.edu.cn/News/1c222af5384849b3980547c9d6878939.htm)). The first "Junction China" flagship was set for **3 to 5 Dec 2021 in Chengdu**, with Nokia Shanghai Bell, Audi, Roblox and Urbstror as track partners and a target of 300+ hackers, but it was listed as **"Postponed – New dates TBC"** and never confirmed ([FinnCham Beijing](https://fbcbj.glueup.cn/event/junction-china-2021-postponed-new-dates-tbc-34268)). **Junction China 2023** did run in Chengdu and had finished by about 2 Oct 2023. Junction described it as a platform for "university students all around China" to work with leading tech companies ([Junction LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hackjunction_how-junction-impacts-the-innovative-atmosphere-activity-7114641503045451778-QRZu)). No partners or winners for 2023 were found.

The record has gaps: nothing found for 2019, 2022, 2024 or 2025, no participant counts before 2026, and no published winners for any Junction China edition. The organizer has stayed the same. **Topi Guo (郭海峰)**, who uses a hackjunction.com address, was the 2021 contact and wrote Junction's 2023 article about the Chengdu event ([FinnCham Beijing](https://fbcbj.glueup.cn/event/junction-china-2021-postponed-new-dates-tbc-34268); [Junction Medium](https://hackjunction.medium.com/how-junction-impacts-the-innovative-atmosphere-of-the-young-tech-talents-in-china-8d5b61e314a4)). The host city follows the local government sponsor: Chengdu's Tianfu New Area through 2023, then Beijing's Zhongguancun in 2026. A future edition could land in either city or somewhere new.

## Junction China is a feeder for Helsinki, one year out

Junction runs two kinds of satellite events. **JunctionX** lets local teams, often student groups, run a hackathon under the Junction brand in their own city ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junction_(hackathon))). Regional flagships are run with the core team or a close partner. Junction Asia in Korea, for example, is run with the Seoul non-profit Shift and the City of Busan ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/www/tech/2022/05/133_328528.html)). Junction China belongs to the second kind. The site says it is "officially launched by Junction", Junction Oy is a named co-organizer, and its winners go straight to the Finnish flagship ([hackjunction.cn](https://hackjunction.cn)). Korea shows how far that path can go: team 고진감래 won Junction Asia 2022 and then **won Junction 2022 overall in Finland** ([NewsSeoul](https://newsseoul.co.kr/news/view/1065568819434158)).

The timing matters. **Junction 2026** runs **13 to 15 Nov 2026** in Espoo in the Helsinki metro area, with about **2,000 participants** and a **€100k** prize pool ([Espoo events](https://www.espoo.fi/en/events/espooevents:agp767vqb4); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/12-hackathons-worth-your-fall-and-winter)). Its applications are already closed. Sources give the deadline as 25 Oct or 6 Sep 2026 ([2026.hackjunction.com](https://2026.hackjunction.com/); [hackjunction.app](https://hackjunction.app/hackathons/junction-2026)). Junction China 2026 ended a week before Junction 2026, so its winners qualify for **Junction 2027**, not this year's event. For Hour 49, the China route to Helsinki is the long way round. A team in Cluj can apply to Junction 2027 directly, and Junction 2026 offered international participants **€300/€100 travel grants** (search snippet only, no primary page). Winning in China only helps a team that wants the China event for its own sake: the partner companies, the robotics and wearables hardware, and the Beijing–Finland network.

The participant mix also argues against treating Junction China as an easy entry. Its audience is Chinese students from Tsinghua, Peking University, BUPT and 20+ other universities ([Sina Finance / CNS](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-08/doc-inixyrxh7498525.shtml)). It is promoted on an expat events list as an "International Hackathon" ([bovi/beijing-events](https://github.com/bovi/beijing-events/issues/7)), yet it had only seven foreign teams. Hour 49 would be one of a handful of international teams competing with strong local teams on Chinese partner hardware and APIs. The working language was never stated. The site is bilingual, so expect Chinese first with English support.

## A Cluj team can reach Beijing cheaply, but the visa window closes 31 Dec 2026

**Visa.** Romanian ordinary-passport holders can currently enter China **visa-free for up to 30 days** for business, tourism, family visits, exchange visits or transit ([NIA](https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147463/c183390/content.html)). A hackathon fits "business/exchange". The Romanian MFA says other purposes still need a visa ([financialintelligence.ro citing MAE](https://financialintelligence.ro/mae-saluta-eliminarea-temporara-a-obligativitatii-vizei-pentru-romanii-care-calatoresc-in-china/)). The policy is **confirmed only until 24:00 Beijing time on 31 Dec 2026**, and no extension for Europeans had been announced as of 31 Oct 2026 ([Bliss Travel](https://blisstravel.life/blogs/travel-guide/china-visa-free-when-does-it-end); [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/news/europeans-can-now-visit-china-visa-free-until-the-end-of-2026-heres-what-that-means-for-your-next-trip-013026)). The only 2027 extension found so far covers Russia ([CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-05-20/China-extends-visa-free-policy-for-Russia-until-end-of-2027-1NiFHg4DlzG/share_amp.html)). Last year's extension was announced on 3 Nov, so watch for an announcement in November and December 2026. Without one, a 2027 trip needs an **M (business) or F (exchange) visa** from the Chinese Visa Application Service Center in Bucharest. It takes only EEA-issued personal bank cards, and its fee page was unreachable during research ([visaforchina BUH](https://visaforchina.cn/BUH2_EN/generalinformation/visaknowledge/263487.shtml)). For an M visa, ask the organizers for an invitation letter. The **240-hour visa-free transit** also applies to Romanians, but only with a confirmed onward ticket to a third country, such as Bucharest to Beijing to Seoul ([chinafortravelers](https://chinafortravelers.com/tools/240-hour-transit/eligible/)).

**Flights.** Since **4 Sep 2026**, Air China flies **Bucharest to Beijing nonstop three times a week** (Mon/Wed/Fri), about 10 hours each way. It is the first direct Romania–China route since 2003 ([Agerpres](https://agerpres.ro/english/2026/08/11/starting-september-4-romanians-will-be-able-to-fly-directly-to-china-three-times-a-week--1584068); [Beijing gov](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/latest/news/202609/t20260906_4852259.html)). Fares were quoted "from 700 euros" before launch ([Romania Insider](https://www.romania-insider.com/bucharest-beijing-direct-flights-aug-2026)). For a Beijing event, the simplest route is Cluj to Bucharest and then this flight. From Cluj itself, Turkish Airlines via Istanbul is the usual cheapest one-stop option, at roughly **USD 270–950 one way** ([Rome2rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Shanghai/Cluj-Napoca); [Trip.com](https://www.trip.com/flights/cluj-napoca-to-shanghai/airfares-clj-sha)). No live late-2026 fares were checked.

**Costs on the ground.** A backpacker day in a major Chinese city costs about **USD 25–50 (CNY 170–350)** including a hostel bed, and metro rides cost CNY 3–6 ([travelofchina](https://www.travelofchina.com/how-much-does-a-day-in-shanghai-cost-2026/); [chinavigators](https://www.chinavigators.com/?p=3570)). The hackathon provides meals, so the real costs are flights and lodging on the nights before and after. Our own estimate, built from those figures, is about **EUR 250–500 per person for a week, plus EUR 600–1,000 return airfare**. The 2026 edition's travel support was explicitly for participants from outside Beijing, Tianjin and Hebei, which in principle includes foreigners. Its amounts were never published, so email info@hackjunction.cn before booking anything.

**Internet and payments.** This affects a hackathon team more than a tourist. Google, OpenAI and Claude are not reliably reachable from Chinese networks, and GitHub is unreliable. A **roaming SIM or foreign eSIM** routes traffic abroad and avoids the VPN grey area. Venue and hotel Wi-Fi put you back behind the firewall ([intotravelchina](https://intotravelchina.com/travel-guide/do-i-need-a-vpn-in-china.html)). VPN sites are blocked inside China, so any VPN must be installed before departure ([comparitech](https://www.comparitech.com/blog/vpn-privacy/are-vpns-legal-in-china)). Clone repositories and download models, packages and Docker images before flying. Plan to use Chinese model APIs, which the Xiaomi MiMo track will probably expect anyway. For payments, **Alipay and WeChat Pay accept foreign Visa and Mastercard after passport verification**. Several guides cite a 3% fee on payments over CNY 200, and per-transaction limits vary by source, so check the limit shown in the app ([mychina.guide](https://mychina.guide/questions/foreigners-use-alipay-wechat-pay-china-international-credit-cards); [travelofchina](https://www.travelofchina.com/alipay-wechat-new-5000-limit-for-foreigners-2026/)). Tell your bank before the trip.

**Funding.** The research found no Junction China grant aimed at European teams. In order, the likely sources are the organizer's own travel support, sponsorship from companies in Cluj, and UBB faculty or student-association funds. Comparable Chinese events such as AdventureX in Hangzhou have reimbursed flights for distant participants ([adventure-x.org](https://adventure-x.org/en)). UBB funding and Erasmus+ short-mobility rules for China were not researched and need checking with UBB's international office.

## Conclusion

For Hour 49, Junction China is worth watching, not chasing. Its prize money is small and its Helsinki ticket gives nothing a Cluj team can't get by applying to Junction 2027 directly. Its real value is access the European circuit doesn't offer: government-backed Beijing organizers, Chinese robotics and AR hardware companies as challenge setters, and a Finland–Beijing matchmaking program built for exactly the kind of cross-border team Hour 49 would be. With only seven foreign teams in 2026, the organizers appear to want more international entrants, and a Romanian team would stand out.

The deciding factor is timing, and none of it is in the team's control yet. The China edition has skipped years before, has moved cities with its sponsors, and has no 2027 date. The visa-free window that would make a trip simple ends on 31 Dec 2026. The practical next steps: email info@hackjunction.cn now to ask about a 2027 edition, international eligibility and travel support, and to get on the mailing list. Watch for China's visa-free extension announcement before New Year. Treat Junction 2027 in Helsinki as the main target, and keep the Bucharest–Beijing nonstop flight in mind if a China date appears.
