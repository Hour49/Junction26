# Hook guide: styles, rules, real examples

A hook is the first thing a passing hacker hears at our table. It has one job: make them stop and want the next sentence. In peer review they compare us with the table they just saw, so the hook must be **easy to repeat** to a friend an hour later.

## Rules for every hook
- 4–12 words. One breath. No commas if you can help it.
- Say it out loud before you keep it. If you trip, cut it.
- Plain words only (see [plain-words.md](plain-words.md)).
- Any number must come from `04-pitch/core.md` or `03-research/sources.md`, with its label (measured / reported / assumed). No source → use a non-number style instead.
- Never promise something the demo cannot show.

## The styles (write at least one of each; 8–10 hooks total)

| Style | Shape | When it works best |
|---|---|---|
| **Question** | "Ever <everyday annoying thing>?" | Problem everyone has felt. Gets a nod or a laugh |
| **X for Y** | "<famous thing> for <new user/job>" | When a well-known product makes the idea click. Pick something non-tech people know (Google Maps, Shazam, a smoke alarm, autocorrect), not a startup only devs know |
| **Surprising number** | "<number + unit people feel> <happens>. We stop it." | When we have a strong, sourced number |
| **Everyday scene** | "You open the bag. The milk isn't there." | Paints a picture in 2–3 tiny sentences. Best for non-experts |
| **Blunt opinion** | "No one likes <thing>." | Fast, a bit funny, honest. Needs a quick follow-up line |
| **Promise / dare** | "Give us 30 seconds and you'll <see X>." | When the demo wow is truly visual |
| **Before → after** | "From <pain> to <result> in <time>." | When the change is easy to measure |
| **Persona** | "Meet <name>. <Their job>. <Their pain>." | When the user is very specific. Use a made-up first name *labelled as an example*, never a fake real person |

**Pick which to test first:** mark 3 hooks "TEST FIRST", from 3 different styles. Prefer hooks that (1) a non-tech person gets, (2) name the user, (3) point at what the demo shows in the next 30 s.

## What the 2025 winners actually said (auto-captions, wording approximate)
From `02-reference/pitch-transcripts/`. Use as patterns, never copy.

- **LoreDash, 2nd overall, submission video**: question + everyday scene. "Have you ever gone down the rabbit hole by clicking the random article button on Wikipedia?" Then a funny escalation ("…hours later, you're reading about…"), then the identity: every article becomes a game. Lesson: start in a moment everyone has lived.
- **Wavelet, 1st overall, submission video**: humour + promise. "Why? Cuz we're insane. If you want to see the coolest graphics you've ever seen, stick around." Then, after the details, a plain reset: "Okay, you get the gist. We built a radio wave simulator." Lesson: a dare buys attention, and a 5-word plain identity line saves you when the tech gets heavy.
- **Walkthrough, top 5, stage**: blunt opinion + surprising number. "No one likes grocery stores." Then "around one and a half to two hours weekly just wandering around the aisles". Lesson: opinion first, number second.
- **λpump, top 5, stage**: everyday scene + persona. "Right now, beneath our feet, an entire system is handling everything we flush away…" then "I want you to meet my friend. He's Jamie. He manages a wastewater treatment facility." Lesson: make a hidden, boring system feel close and human.
- **DroneStalker, top 5, stage**: opened with its own AI voice roasting the user, which got a laugh. Lesson: if the product has a voice, let it do the hook.

## Analogies (2–3 in the output)
- Compare to something from home, the street or the shop, not to other software.
- Shape: "It's like <everyday thing> for <their problem>: <why the match works in 6 words>."
- Good sources: smoke alarm (warns before), spell-check (catches it while you type), sat-nav rerouting (finds another way), a shop assistant who says "we're out, but try this".
- Check it doesn't promise more than we built.

## A/B test card (for `/easy-pitch test`)
- 3 hooks, 3 different styles, labelled A, B, C.
- Say only the hook, then stop for 2 seconds. Watch the face.
- Signs it worked: they ask "how?" / they repeat it back / they laugh or nod / they lean in to the screen.
- Signs it failed: "sorry, what?" / blank face / they ask "so what is it?".
- Rotate A → B → C → A across peers so each gets 3–5 tries. Don't change the wording mid-test.
