# Experiments: what we tried on 2026-11-14 and what it gave

On the night of 13-14 November the team ran about a dozen experiment tracks on the CubiCasa5K raster sheets (the
part of the Stoa dataset where we read a drawing from pixels). This page lists every arm worth naming, what it
changed, what it scored, whether it shipped, and what it cost. It is the "how we improved" record that
[GOLDEN.md](GOLDEN.md) points at. Final as of 2026-11-14 09:14: every track is closed (the last arm, integ-5, closed at
08:59) and no GPU machine is running.

## Protocol

- **Split.** `stoa_eval exp-split` takes the 3,828 CubiCasa images that have dev questions, drops the 69 golden v2
  dev files, and cuts the rest by drawing group into train (3,007 images), val (376) and test (376). It is seeded, so a
  rerun writes the same files. The sealed eval split never enters. An audit re-ran the split and found no overlap by
  file or by group with golden v2 or the eval split.
- **Every arm is an A/B on val.** Both arms answer the same 6,764 val questions through the backend's own question
  parser and takeoff, judged by Stoa's rule (counts exact; a missing image counts as wrong). The unit is the image.
  The reported difference is B minus A in percentage points with a 95% bootstrap interval over images (10,000
  resamples). B wins only when the interval is above 0. An exact McNemar p is printed too, but it counts questions,
  and the English and Finnish questions on one image are not independent, so we do not cite it.
- **References on val.** Gold answers 100.0%; the train prior (the answer prior refit on train questions only) 55.9%.
  The prior is strong on some room families (kitchen 96.2%, bathroom 86.8%) and weak on elements (door 8.6%, window
  14.1%).
- **Test is for the one arm we ship, read once.** The CubiCasa test split was read once for the shipped config C (07:47,
  `stoa_eval ab --part test --certify`, recorded in `test-certified.json`): 85.0% exact (5708/6718), 95% CI [83.4,
  86.5] over images, against 56.3% for the train prior (+28.7 pp [+26.7, +30.8]). Nothing was tuned after it. One
  deviation, stated plainly: the segmentation track scored its arm Bl on the test split at 06:01 (`test_Bl.log`),
  before that read and against the read-once rule. Bl did not ship and no decision used that run.
- **Golden v2 is the end-to-end look, never a tuning set.** Golden v2's 79 CubiCasa files (69 dev, 10 held out) are
  scored with the whole pipeline, once per frozen config, and nothing is picked from them.
- **Tuned on val means optimistic.** Thresholds, family choices and prompts were picked on val in most tracks. Where
  the picking happened on the same pages the interval is computed on, the table says so. Where an arm reads gold at
  answer time (oracles, used only as upper bounds), it is marked and never picked.
- **Gemini and Qwen may have seen CubiCasa5K.** The dataset has been public since 2019. Every number that depends on
  a hosted or open VLM carries this caveat.
- **Seed noise is about 2 points.** The same YOLO26 M recipe with seed 1 scored -1.6 pp against seed 0 (interval
  [-2.5, -0.6]). The interval covers the pages, not the training seed, so a difference under about 2 pp between two
  separate trainings is a fact about those two checkpoints, not about the recipe.

Sources. The track logs live in each person's local `.agents/exp/` (gitignored, not in this repo): `leaderboard.md`
(generated by `stoa_eval leaderboard`, 2026-11-14 04:30), `detector.md`, `region.md`, `decide.md`, `decide2.md`,
`ocr.md`, `seg.md`, `vlm.md`, `rl.md`, `cvx.md`, `photo.md`, `areas.md`, `agent.md`, `eu.md`, `integ.md`,
`audit.md` and `verda-ledger.md`. Each table below names its log. Costs come from the ledger and from each log's own
money section. Hosted-model costs are in USD as billed; GPU rental is in EUR.

## How to read the numbers

"Exact" is the share of val questions answered exactly right, per family or overall. The detector tracks read
elements and plans only, so on all 6,764 questions they are capped by the prior's room answers (or by nothing, where
the arm leaves rooms unanswered and the room questions count wrong). That is why the leaderboard shows most detector
arms as "no difference" against the prior on all questions, while `detector.md` shows every element family above the
prior. The two logs agree once the denominator is taken into account; this page gives the family-level numbers where a
track log has them.

The leaderboard (04:30) predates the later detector sizes (M, L, X, the 1536 variants of M and L) and the late EU, photo
and areas arms, so a few best-per-family lines in it are out of date. `detector.md` is current for the detector.

right for the dataset's answers and wrong for a person asking. It is only for a rooms source that reads labels.

count-level gate: with seven readers, cross-fit on val, no family gains 5 points of coverage at 97, 98 or 99% exact, so
it is used only to point. Val, config C1's 1,637 door and fixture answers, 170 of them wrong, judged against the gold
boxes; "not sure" is the 392 answers the count-level gate leaves out at its operating point. Six other readers at k = 2
(the rule fixed before the results) put a highlight on an error of 85 of the 170 wrong answers with 1.16 highlights per
not-sure answer, but all six cost an estimated 15 to 25 s of CPU per page (not timed). The set to ship was chosen for
its cost, not for its val row: the detector's first view (free, it is part of the eight-view run), a second detector in
one view (not timed) and the segmentation network's icons (2.9 to 5.7 s per page), at k = 1, at most 5 places per
answer. Through the backend function that set lists an error of 93 of the 170 wrong answers (95 without the cap) and
107 of the 260 error marks; 19% of the places it lists are errors; a not-sure answer gets 1.39 places on average and
99 of the 392 get none. The wrong answers it does not reach are errors every reader makes alike (a doorway without a
leaf), which no disagreement shows. Listing the strongest disputes first is worth nothing measurable on val: the first
five in reading order reach 94 of 170. Log: `think-local.md`.

## What shipped (the demo config)

All of it is selected by settings, so each piece can be switched off:

- **Elements and plans (config C, PR #102):** YOLO26 L at 1024 px, one view, with Gemini's count swap on shower+bathtub
  and sauna only (`decisions-s1536:detector:C-yolo26l`). The region filter is dropped for elements because its
  thresholds were fit on the 1536 detector. Thresholds are the all-val ones. Single test read: 85.0% [83.4, 86.5].
- **Sure flag (PR #98):** the gate certified on that test run, at least 96.2% of sure plans exact.
- **Rooms:** the Gemini whole-page reader (G4) with rooms outside the region model's predicted flat dropped (`regionrooms`). Kitchen
  questions stay with the prior, which beats every reader there.
- **Ingest signal:** Gemini's photo flag at P >= 0.9, with the probe as the fallback if the call fails.
- **Scale:** `RASTER_SCALE=priors` (door-width prior, page-size prior) is on in the demo config (PR #82; `tools/qa-env.sh config`) and off only as the code default. Estimates are always labelled.
- **Behind a setting, default off:** the EU open-model route (PR #103, -1.9 pp [-2.8, -1.0] on val), the photo switch
  (PR #95, no better than C on photos with gold).
- **Multi-view and region windows at runtime:** the `region:<arm>` source runs eight views with per-plan windows for
  `C-yolo26s-nms` and one view for `C-yolo26s-1536`; those are the only two arms in `raster_region/arms.json`, so
  `region:` refuses the M and L detectors. The `detector:` source reads one view. Eight views of L (+2.8 pp [+1.8, +3.8]
  against the old 1536 arm on val; 2719/2948 on golden with precomputed rooms) therefore exist only as precomputed rows.

## What didn't work and why

- **GRPO on the VLM counter.** Two arms, -0.6 and -0.35 pp against the supervised parent, intervals spanning 0. The reward
  went up only to the level greedy decoding already had, and the remaining errors are page-level, which a per-tile
  reward on 500 tiles cannot see.
- **VLMs as counters.** The best (fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B with a rotation vote) is 4.4 pp behind the detector on all
  questions; zero-shot VLMs, OWLv2 and SAM 3 are far worse. More training was the only big lever, and it plateaued near
  52%.
- **Detect-then-verify.** The verifier raises AUROC for windows and doors by a few points and still lowers exact counts
  (W1 -2.4 pp). A detector trained on 3,007 pages already ranks its own boxes about as well as a verifier fitted on a few
  hundred.
- **Region filters beyond the first one.** The learned region head inside the segmentation network (H) marked most of
  the page as annotated; overlap rules for the room-box filter (ov25, ov50) were neutral or worse on the held-out half;
  inside-plan and inside-room box filters on the detector did nothing; the room class added to the detector lost 0.3 pp.
- **Window box merging and splitting.** Merging panes loses; splitting long components by length gains nothing; a
  perfect per-sheet choice of pane or whole-window granularity would be worth at most 7 pp of windows. The gold changes
  convention from sheet to sheet, and nothing post-hoc fixes that. Windows stayed at about 45-55% in every track. The last attempt, a page-level predictor of the labelling convention,
  reached val AUROC 0.702 and was killed below its 0.75 line.
- **Resolution and size.** 1536 px helped S and not M or L; 2048 px lost; X over L gained nothing; a 72-pass L was not
  run, since val mAP was still rising only slowly.
- **Seeds and recipe noise.** A second seed of the same recipe moved the score by 1.6 pp. Several "wins" and "losses"
  under 2 pp between separate trainings are therefore noise.
- **Gemini-only variants.** Higher image resolution and robotics-ER gave no gain; the whole-page orientation reader is
  worse than the OCR vote; the answerability and conflict-triage gates add only false refusals.
- **Template matching** (normalised cross-correlation, with and without DINOv2) cannot count doors and windows because
  lengths vary and the symbols fire along walls.
- **Confidence gates at val size.** The Learn-then-Test certificate opens only for plans, because the 95% bound needs
  about 60 plans per error. On the first gate (S1536) elements and rooms stayed shut, because Gemini is at least 0.95 sure
  of a few wrong counts. The refit for config C opens elements on val at 0.771, but the test certificate is on plans, the
  unit, and rooms stay shut.

## Gaps and caveats (final)

- **Test.** Read once for config C (85.0% [83.4, 86.5]). Every other val number above comes from the best of many
  checkpoints, thresholds and family choices on the same 376 pages and is optimistic. The segmentation track's Bl test
  run (06:01) broke the read-once rule; it did not ship and no decision used it. Per-class intervals on test are wide for
  rare classes (29 bathtubs, 14 saunas).
- **Missing intervals.** Rows with no interval are marked as such or give per-family exact rates from the log: the first
  OCR and seg rows, the cvx template-matching rows (task closed before `ab` ran) and the pass-12 detector rows. Where a number has only a question-level p (McNemar), it is marked and not used.
- **Missing costs.** The D-FINE arm and the photo arms have no per-arm cost (photo: 3.05 EUR for the whole track); areas costs nothing. GPU rental in all: 53.76 EUR, from the provider's
  balance (200.00 EUR at the first check at 02:08, 146.24 EUR at 08:58). The ledger's per-track lines sum to about 55.8 EUR
  (detector 20.2, seg 8.3, vlm 7.6, eu 10.2, photo 3.05, rl 2.2, serve 1.8, cvx 1.6, ocr 0.6, region 0.2), so they overstate
  the bill by about 2 EUR; they are estimates. Hosted-model calls, summed from the lines the logs give: decide 1.81 USD,
  decide2 0.97, ocr (including the arm G rooms reader on val, about 2.35, and its golden reads) about 3.1, agent 13.31, the
  test read of config C about 2.47, the live photo tests about 0.68, golden-18's runtime rooms reads about 0.44, integ-6's
  cache-race check about 0.31: about 23.1 USD in all. The EU track logs no Gemini spend, and the integ golden runs reuse
  cached replies.
- **Where logs disagree.** (1) The leaderboard and the track logs score different denominators (see "How to read the
  numbers"). (2) C-yolo26s-1536 is 57.0% in the leaderboard (torch) and 57.1% in region's re-run on the ONNX export; the
  runtime reads the ONNX file. (3) The areas log carried -20.9 [-26.5, -15.6] for C-tot against C; a re-run, and the
  audit, give -20.2 [-25.6, -15.0]; the page uses the re-run. (4) The OCR G and G2 rows were measured with the
  de-duplication bug; the G4 rows are from the fixed parser. (5) The leaderboard's best-per-family list (04:30) predates
  the M, L and X detectors; `detector.md` is current. (6) R1-perplan-multi-v8 and the 1536 detector were never compared
  paired on val.
- **Oracles.** Twelve arm folders read gold at answer time (the cvx oracle rows, region/oracle-gold, ocr/*-reg,
  areas/D2-oracle-goldrooms). The leaderboard marks them and the policy never picks them. An audit read the logs and
  code of every arm in the answer policy and found no arm that reads gold at predict time.
- **Leakage.** Gemini and Qwen may have seen CubiCasa5K. Ten of the 79 golden-v2 CubiCasa images are held-out
  files and were sent to the hosted model, on approval, for inference only; nothing was fitted on them.
- **Tuned numbers.** The agent's family scope and routing rule, the decide swap thresholds, the verifier thresholds
  and the detected-door constant in the scale arms were picked on val; the logs say so, and so does this page.
