Worker lit-detect-b, read-only. Date read for every source: 2026-11-14 unless a row says otherwise. Reading log with one
line per source: `lit-detect-b-log.md`. Marks: **V** = I read it on the page named (full text or
the table cited); **R** = reported by the authors, not re-checked; **E** = my estimate. Leakage flag = was the

## 1. Top 5 (transfers and floor-plan work in one list)

1. **RF-DETR fine-tuned on engineering drawings in 768 px overlapping tiles, with containment-aware merge of tile
   duplicates** (railway signal-layout drawings, DSC, Sensors Oct 2026). 57 training drawings cut to 4,813 patches of
   768x768 (stride 384), RF-DETR-Seg-Large on one RTX 4090, at most 100 epochs, batch 4 x 7 accumulation; test on 10
   drawings with 1,755 instances: precision 0.8994, recall 0.9470, F1 0.9226. Their new part is the merge: box NMS,
   then mask intersection-over-minimum 0.6, which cut false positives 288 to 186 on identical candidates. **Verdict:
   try** (as the recipe for our tiling, merge and augmentation; the detector itself is the RF-DETR the earlier reports
   already ranked). Reason: the one 2026 paper that does our job on drawings with an Apache detector, few images, one
   consumer GPU, and it reports ablations over three seeds (V, Tables 5-12). What the tables say that we can use:
   - Without a merge step the tiled output is mostly duplicates: precision 0.345 (3,206 false positives, 65 misses);
     box NMS 0.500; box NMS + box containment 0.853 (288 FP); DSC (box NMS + mask containment) 0.899 (186 FP) with
     recall 0.963 -> 0.947. Overlap barely matters: F1 0.9176 at 0%, 0.9226 at 20%, 0.9147 at 25%, 0.9169 at 50%.
   - A cheap augmentation that matters: **RandomShift**, translate the training content by up to +-12 px so every
     residue modulo the ViT patch stride (12 px) occurs: Box AP 0.6630 -> 0.6882 (three seeds, sd 0.002). The effect
     is on small objects whose position relative to the patch grid changes the features. Their RF-DETR-Seg
     configuration has a 12-px patch stride. I read the RF-DETR source (`src/rfdetr/config.py`, GitHub API, 2026-11-14):
     detection N/S/M/L use patch size 16 (the older "Base" 14) at 384/512/576/704 px and a single feature level
     (`projector_scale = ["P4"]`), the Seg models use patch size 12 at 312-768 px. So the shift range for a detection
     RF-DETR is +-16 px; for a CNN/HGNetv2 detector such as D-FINE the analogue is a shift up to its coarsest stride
     (32 px), not tested anywhere I read.
   - The same config file is a warning for our 5-20 px symbols: with a 16-px patch and one stride-16 level, a symbol
     smaller than one patch must be made larger by resolution (a 855-px plan resized to 512 px puts a 10-px window
     cell at 6 px, under half a patch). The configs accept a custom `resolution` (positional grid is rescaled in
     code), and DSC trained at 768 px with the Seg-L config whose default is 504. Memory and time then grow with the
     square of the resolution (E).
   - **Directed Copy-Paste** of rare classes into blank training patches: Box AP 0.6336 -> 0.6630, and the rarest class
     (314 instances in the set) Mask AP 0.2536 -> 0.6176 (single run, V Table 11). We have rare classes too (bathtub
     312, sauna stove 2.5k boxes).
   - A Wasserstein term (the NWD idea, gated to compact boxes) adds Box AP 0.6630 -> 0.6679 alone; the full bundle
     (K=4 sampling points, weight surgery, RandomShift, Wasserstein) reaches 0.7045.
   - Honest comparison: on their validation patches YOLO11-seg-L (AGPL, out for us) has Box AP 0.7228 vs 0.6336 for the
     unmodified RF-DETR-Seg-L (V, Table 8, descriptive): a DETR is not automatically better on drawings.
   Caveat: code, weights and drawings are not public (V, Data Availability); single-lab numbers; segmentation task. Related findings from the repo and later rounds: query caps (T15), patch-grid confidence aliasing (T25), SAHI default merge (T24).
   Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13709312/ (Europe PMC full text, read 2026-11-14), MDPI DOI
   10.3390/s26206413, CC BY 4.0.
2. **Train on random crops of the full-resolution page, infer on the whole page: do not pre-cut tiles and merge by
   NMS** (optical music recognition, DAS 2024). Same architecture, full-page-trained vs cropped-and-merged:
   MUSCIMA++ mean AP50 0.849 vs 0.802, mAP 0.642 vs 0.582 (V, Table 1 and text). On DeepScoresV2 (printed scores,
   about 600-660 symbols per page, 3000x2000 px) Cascade R-CNN + FocalNet-B reaches AP50 0.977 / mAP 0.902 on 136
   classes; DINO (a DETR) collapses when trained on random crops and tested on whole pages (mean AP50 0.642 vs 0.914
   without cropping, Table 3), because its positional encoding does not survive the change of geometry. **Verdict:
   try, as a rule, not a model**: whatever DETR we fine-tune (RF-DETR, D-FINE) must see the same tile geometry in
   training and inference; RF-DETR and D-FINE both stretch to a square, so pad a non-square page instead of whole-page stretching (T26). Source: https://hal.science/hal-04268139v2/document (read 2026-11-14), DAS 2024.
3. **Dome-DETR** (aerial tiny dense objects, ACM MM 2025). D-FINE plus a density head that sets the number of queries
   and sparse window attention. AI-TOD-V2 test at 800x800: mean object 12.7 px, 86% under 16 px, 24.6 objects per
   image: **the closest published numbers to our images**. Dome-DETR-S 33.3 AP / APvt 17.8 vs D-FINE-S 30.1 / 14.0;
   L: 34.6 vs 31.3 (V, Table 1, authors' re-implemented baselines). Code Apache-2.0, weights "academic research only",
   trained on aerial data (leakage: no, and no use to us). Cost: 160 epochs, total batch 8, 4090-class GPUs, training
   time not stated (V); open issues on slow convergence (#41) and a defunct loss (#50). Note the gotcha: every D-FINE
   baseline in their table is "re-implemented with 4 feature maps" (V), i.e. with a stride-4 level; stock D-FINE has
   3 levels (strides 8, 16, 32, V config file) and 25 GFLOPs for S at 640 px (V README), against 112 GFLOPs for their
   4-level S* at 800x800. So the 30.1 AP baseline already includes the stride-4 level that stock D-FINE lacks, and the
   price of that level (and of the larger input) is about 4.5x FLOPs (my arithmetic from two V numbers). For a 5-px
   symbol a stride-8 cell is larger than the object: either add the stride-4 level or up-sample the plan 2x; no paper I
   read compares the two on a DETR head to head; the closest are T23 (a plain stride-4 level adds only +0.19 to +0.63 AP on two RT-DETR/ViT-style detectors, abstract-level and COCO/VisDrone, not drawings) and T28/T30/T31 (higher input resolution beat slicing and helped small objects without retraining). **Verdict: maybe, as an idea source, not the first run** (revised after
   the repository rounds): a plain stride-4 level adds little (T23), D-FINE fine-tunes are riskier on small sets (T27) and Dome-DETR is not reproduced on small batches (T18), so run higher input resolution on RF-DETR first (T28, T31); a D-FINE-S/M with a stride-4 level is the second experiment, and the density-to-queries head only if the query cap (T15) bites. Source:
   https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05741v2 and https://github.com/RicePasteM/Dome-DETR.
4. **Centre-only, fixed-size detection head** (YOLSO, historical map symbols, ICDAR 2023). A padding-free
   fully-convolutional net predicts, per grid cell, class and centre offset, no box size; a count is the number of
   peaks. 22,882 labelled points, 4k x 4k maps, point mAP@0.5 97.9% vs YOLOv3 43.6% and Faster R-CNN 70.2% (V,
   Table 1). Code GPL-3.0 (idea only), data on request. **Verdict: maybe**, as a second counter for stage 8 (an
   independent measurement). Caveat: assumes one fixed scale; our scale varies by 10x across images.
   Source: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/198859/1/_ICDAR_YOLSO.pdf, https://github.com/waps101/YOLSO.
5. **Synthetic-over-real-scan augmentation, "ScoreAug"** (music object detection on real scans, TISMIR 2023). Draw
   clean synthetic scores over 51 real blank scanned pages (keep the darker pixel), add small rotations, flips,
   brightness and blur: AP@0.25 on 12 real scanned scores (12,553 symbols) goes 36.0% -> 56.5%, and 73.7% after 200
   extra epochs on full pages (V, Table 3; the abstract says 73.3); the clean synthetic test set drops 87.6% -> 83.3%.
   A gradient-reversal domain adaptation only reaches 48.9%. **Verdict: try, for any synthetic plan we add** (ResPlan
   renders): the closest measured sim-to-real recipe for drawings, with real blank paper as the noise source. Caveat:
   S2A-Net oriented detector, one small test set, code on GitHub without a licence file (idea only).
   Source: https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/28719/1/2023_Tuggener-etal_Real-world-music-object-recognition_TISMIR.pdf
   (read 2026-11-14). Also considered and left in the table (row 5): NWD/RFLA tiny-box matching, which only helps CNN
   detectors with anchors (AI-TOD AP 11.1 -> 17.8 for Faster R-CNN, V).

**Late-round addition to the Top 5 (not renumbered, E).** The repository and 2026-paper rounds produced one finding that should sit above items 4 and 5 by evidence quality: **resolution first, slicing second** (T28 RF-DETR-L on small pollinators, hidden test, 1344 px .405 vs sliced YOLO .368; T30 seven CNN detectors, resolution beats tiling with box fusion unless the image exceeds trainable size; T31 frozen RF-DETR-L evaluated at 2.5x pixels +0.038 AP, largest gain on small objects). It is a rule, not a model, none of the three is on drawings, and T30 is CNN-only, which is why it is not a ranked method; if the list is cut to three, take items 1 and 2 and this rule. Three count hazards travel with it: query cap (T15), patch-grid confidence flips (T25), merge rules (T24, T30).

**The scope question, this angle only.** Which Apache/MIT detector for about 3.8k images, 12 GB, under 3 hours: no
transfer source settles it; the measured evidence says (a) DETR-family detectors (D-FINE, RF-DETR, RT-DETR) beat YOLO
CNNs on small-object AP by a wide margin when fine-tuned on small datasets (AP_S 34-37 vs 5-7 on RF100-VL, T12), (b)
the two Apache DETRs are within about 2.5 AP_S of each other (D-FINE ahead on RF100-VL and on COCO S/M, T35), and (c) the lever that the drawing papers show is not the
detector but the pipeline around it: resolution that makes the symbol at least a patch/cell wide (T1, T9), the same
tile geometry in training and inference (Top 2), RandomShift and copy-paste augmentation (Top 1), a containment merge
that keeps window panes separate (T3), and centre-hit evaluation for counting (T13). Input resolution and slicing used
by papers on drawings: 768 px tiles at 20% overlap (railway drawings), 1344x800 whole-sheet resize (AEC layout),
1500 px patches at 50% overlap (P&ID), 1000x500 random crops of 3000x2000 pages (music), 640 px for the YOLO baselines.
Tiny-object papers train at 800x800 (Dome-DETR). I found no measurement of the accuracy cost of shrinking the input
for sub-16-px objects other than the up-sampling gains in T9.
Added after the repository rounds (V from issues, E for the conclusions): (d) RF-DETR M/L is the safer fine-tune than D-FINE/DEIM in a short budget, because D-FINE and DEIM have open reports of non-convergence and seed instability on small sets (T27) and Dome-DETR needs 120-160 epochs and is not reproduced on small batches (T18); (e) the only hours datum for an Apache DETR is RF-DETR Seg-L at 648 px on a V100: about 4 min 14 s for 400 micro-batches of 2 images, about 5 minutes per 1,000 images per epoch, 4.9 GB peak during training, and a validation out-of-memory in mask matching that release 1.11.0 later addressed (T25), so 3 hours of budget is about 30-35 epochs of 1,000 images, or 10 epochs of 3,000, before validation and any tile multiplication (E; DSC trained up to 100 epochs on 4,813 tiles); (f) the one fresh single-GPU RF-DETR vs YOLO comparison on small objects found that resolution (1120 -> 1344 px: .379 -> .405) beat slicing and ensembling (T28); (g) three count hazards sit between the detector and an exact count: the query cap per tile (T15), confidence flips with the patch grid (T25) and merge rules that remove true neighbours (T24).

**What I would run first from this angle (E, synthesis of the notes below, not measured by anyone).**
1. Whatever DETR is fine-tuned (RF-DETR M or D-FINE-S/M, ranked by the other detect workers): train on random crops of
   the whole plan at a resolution that makes the median symbol at least one patch/cell wide, infer on the whole plan,
   tile only the few sheets over about 1,500 px (Top 2, T1, T9). Evidence from the later rounds points the same way: a single higher-resolution pass beat slicing and ensembling for RF-DETR-L (T28) and for CNN detectors (T30), and a checkpoint evaluated at 1.5-2.5x its training pixels, no retraining, improved small objects (T31), so test that on dev first.
2. Add RandomShift up to the coarsest stride, rot90/flip, and copy-paste of the rare classes (bathtub, sauna stove) into
   blank wall regions (Top 1, T-notes); for any synthetic plan add the real-blank-paper overlay (Top 5).
3. Score candidates by centre-match at a distance tolerance and by per-image exact count on dev, not AP50:95 (T13).
4. If tiles are used: class-wise box NMS, then containment merge only across tile borders, and test it on window strips
   before trusting it (T3); SAHI's default merge (GREEDYNMM with IOS) lowers counts on touching neighbours, so use NMS or a border-only merge (T24).
5. YOLSO-style centre head as the independent second counter for stage 8 (Top 4), only after steps 1-3 give a baseline.
6. Guards found in later rounds (E from V facts): choose RF-DETR Medium or Large rather than Nano (T20); count the maximum symbols per tile on dev and keep it well under the query cap, 200 for Seg-M/L and 300 for detection models (T15); log loss curves and run a second seed if D-FINE is used, since one paper reports instability on its task (T16); expect many false boxes from any class-agnostic or synthetic-only first stage, so exact counts need the real-data fine-tune (T14, T17). Also test count stability against tile origin: confidence flips with the patch grid (T25), so use shift TTA or a stride that is not a multiple of the patch period, and fix the confidence threshold on dev with that in place.

## 3. From other fields: the transfer notes

For each: what plays the role of our object, why it should transfer, what would break on our images, and whether code and
weights work as they are.

**T1. Tiny dense symbols from aerial imagery (AI-TOD-V2, VisDrone), Dome-DETR and NWD.**
- Role of our object: a vehicle or ship of 8-16 px = a window cell or a toilet symbol of 5-20 px. AI-TOD-V2 has 24.6 objects per image (sigma 63.9, range 1-2,667), mean size 12.7 px, 86% under 16 px (V, Dome-DETR Sec. 4.1). We have about 20-40 per plan: same density regime, at 800x800 input and no tiling needed for the median image (855 px).
- Evidence: D-FINE-S 30.1 AP, APvt 14.0, APt 30.4 on AI-TOD-V2 (V, Table 1, authors' re-implementation); Dome-DETR adds 3.2-3.3 AP. YOLOv12-S 11.5 AP on the same set (V): a generic CNN YOLO at this size is far behind DETR-family detectors.
- What breaks: the aerial images have texture and context; ours are line art where the symbol is defined by a few strokes touching a wall. The 800 px input assumes the object is 12 px at that scale; a 240-px-long-side plan will have the symbol at 2-4 px unless we up-sample 3x. AP is not exact count: the paper reports no count metric. Dome-DETR queries up to 1,500 per image; we need under 100.
- Usable as is? No. Code is Apache-2.0 and trains from ImageNet HGNetv2; shipped weights are aerial and academic-only. Needs training on our boxes.

**T2. Music symbols on staff lines (DeepScoresV2, MUSCIMA++).**
- Role: a notehead sitting on or between staff lines = a window cell sitting in a wall line; hundreds of tiny same-class symbols per page, many touching.
- What the field did about symbols on lines: it split the class by position (noteheads become `InSpace` and `OnLine`, V, DeepScoresV2 Table III) instead of removing the line. The Deep Watershed Detector cannot detect two objects with the same centre, so the authors dropped staff and ledger lines from it (V, Sec. IV-B). Staff-line removal as a pre-step exists (YOLOv8 paper trains on pages with staff lines removed, V; R_Staff_Net says it helps symbols that overlap lines and little overall, R, abstract only).
- For touching symbols: HRNet Faster R-CNN got AP50 0.799 mAP 0.700 (V, DSV2 Table IV); thin long classes (stems) AP0.5 only 0.013, because axis-aligned boxes of thin slanted objects are mostly background (V). Our windows and doors are axis-aligned rectangles and rot90 multiples, so that failure does not apply; the touching case does.
- **Measured effect of removing the lines** (V, Choi dissertation, Table 2.6/2.7, which re-runs Pacha et al.'s
  detectors on MUSCIMA++): Faster R-CNN Inception-ResNet-v2, 71 classes, mAP 85.12 with staff lines vs 87.80 without
  (weighted mAP 94.68 vs 95.05); per-class AP of the big classes nearly identical (notehead 99.85 vs 99.64, stem 98.82
  vs 98.71, ledger line 97.89 vs 97.40); some small classes move 5-20 points either way (tuplet 30.4 vs 77.1, one run).
  The author concludes the impact "is no longer significant", but notes the staff lines in that dataset are synthetic
  and have no scan distortion. A 2019 detector reaching 99.85 AP on noteheads sitting on lines says that symbols
  touching lines are not the hard part when the box is well defined; the hard parts are thin slanted classes and
  rare classes.
- Extreme size ranges: the music detectors handle symbols from a few pixels (dots) to hundreds (beams, slurs) with
  anchors fitted to the data (Pacha et al., in Choi's thesis: 32x32 grid, stride 8, scales 0.25-4.0, aspect ratios
  0.06-2.2, V) or high-resolution features (HRNet, Cascade R-CNN + FocalNet-B, V DAS 2024); class-specific failure is
  concentrated on thin slanted classes and on rare classes (stems AP0.5 0.013 for HRNet Faster R-CNN, V). Our 8
  classes are compact rectangles of 5-60 px, closer to noteheads and dots than to beams.
- What breaks: staff lines are parallel and removable; our walls are the object's own border (a window is a gap with panes in the wall). Removing walls erases the symbol. The practical transfer is the **class split by context** (window on exterior wall vs window strip between rooms) only if a label split helps recall, which we cannot say without a run; mark E.
- Usable as is? No weights (printed scores). DeepScoresV2 is CC BY 4.0 (V, Zenodo) but of no use for floor plans. The recipe (full-page training, see Top 2) is the transfer.

**T3. Tiling and merge on large drawings (OMR, P&ID, railway signals).**
- Papers on drawings use: 1216 px crops resized to 640 from 3500x2000 pages (V); 1000x500 random crops from 3000x2000 pages trained, whole page at inference (V, best); 1500 px patches with at least 50% overlap from a 4500x7000 resized sheet (V, PID2Graph); SAHI 1280 px with 20% overlap (R, HF card); 768 px patches, stride 384 to train and 614 (20%) to infer (V, DSC).
- Duplicate-merging: DSC shows IoU-based NMS misses duplicates that differ in extent (a symbol cut by a tile edge), and a containment test (intersection over the smaller one, 0.6) after box NMS fixes some (FP 288 -> 186). The OMR paper says the same about cropped inference: IoU thresholds for merging are hard to set (V).
- What breaks: ours are small images, so tiling is only for the few large sheets; the bigger risk is the opposite, symbols under about 6 px. Merging by containment would also merge a window strip's panes (one pane inside a larger "strip" box): the containment threshold is dangerous for adjacent identical symbols. Test on strips before using IoMin.

**T4. Touching, identical neighbours (SKU-110K, microscopy, crowds).**
- SKU-110K: 147 objects per image on average; EM-Merger over Soft-IoU fixes what greedy NMS does to packed identical boxes (AP .413 with NMS -> .482 with merger, count MAE 24.96 -> 23.98, then 14.52 with the full model; Table 3 re-read in full from arxiv.org/html/1904.00853 on 2026-11-14, now V; note EM-Merger alone has RMSE 283.97, a few images fail badly, and PR at IoU .5 rises .491 -> .802). Role: a three-pane window strip. Licence: no licence file on the code repo (gh api 404) so idea only; one-to-one matching in DETR-family heads is the modern way to avoid NMS (DEYOv2 and the HEDS-DETR introduction make the same argument, snippet and page text, R).
- Microscopy: StarDist beats Mask R-CNN on touching pairs (synthetic touching ellipses AP50 .9998 vs .910, V) because boxes of touching objects have near-identical extents; Cellpose-SAM reaches segmentation error rate .163 vs .292 (not AP50) for Cellpose3 on its test set (V). Neither reports a count metric. What breaks: the objects there are blobs; ours are 5-20 px rectangles drawn with 1-px lines, and the pretrained weights are NC.
- Not found: any paper that measures **exact per-image count accuracy** for detectors on touching identical objects. SKU-110K reports MAE per image, not exact-match rate.

**T5. Fixed-size symbols on maps (YOLSO, CaSiDD).**
- Role: a tree symbol of a fixed size on a map tile = a toilet or sink symbol of near-constant size. YOLSO predicts centres only; touching/overlapping trees are one of its listed hard cases (V, Fig. 4 A) and point mAP is 97.9%.
- What breaks: needs a known scale: our symbols range over 5-20 px across plans, and window/door widths vary within a plan. Scale normalisation (resize each plan so the wall thickness or door width is constant) would be a prerequisite; no such step exists yet. Doors with swing arcs are not fixed-size objects.
- Class-agnostic map-sign detection (CaSiDD) reached recall 41.5 at precision 81.1: the author says the instance-boundary and minimal-overlap assumption is often not true for line patterns such as marsh hatching (V). Same risk for hatched or dotted symbols in our plans.

**T6. Circuit and P&ID symbol detection.**
- Masala-CHAI fine-tunes YOLOv8 (AGPL) at 640 px on 4,300 circuit diagrams, 12 classes, and takes nets from a deep Hough line detector after removing components (V, Sec. IV-A); it reports no detector mAP. PID2Graph and the HF card use patching and merging as in T3. Real-sheet symbol mAP50 77.0 (V) is a more honest ceiling than 99.96 on synthetic sheets (R).
- What breaks: P&ID symbols are standard and vector-rendered; floor-plan symbols vary by drafter, as CubiCasa5K labels show. The P&ID numbers are on synthetic sheets, so they say nothing about the style gap we have.

**T7. Mathematical formula and chemical structure symbol detection.** Two searches found handwritten-math YOLO benchmarks (95.17 mAP50 for YOLOv8 on vertical sums, R from search snippet) and nothing on touching symbols; not opened. Updated 2026-11-14: a later search did find chemistry detectors that treat atoms and bonds as boxes (MolMiner, YOLO-based, listing only), all with YOLO/non-commercial licences; see T16. No transfer claimed beyond T16's training-instability caution.

**T8. Synthetic-to-real gap on small symbols (SynthPID, CVPR 2026 workshop; belongs mainly to L8, one line here).**
P&ID symbols "often just 8x8 px" on 7000x4500 sheets, cut into 1500 px patches with 750 stride; a Relationformer
(Deformable DETR + relation head, ResNet-50) trained on 665 synthetic drawings seeded from 12 real ones reaches node
mAP@0.5 64.2 on real OPEN100 vs 67.8 with 12 real drawings (cross-validated) and 71.3 mixing both; the authors say the
gap is structural, not visual (V, https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16513, via web-fetch, 2026-11-14). All on the same 12
images, the authors call it small. What breaks for us: layout structure of floor plans (adjacency of rooms, symbols
inside walls) is the part synthetic plans must copy; code/licence not stated, so no reuse. Pointer: L8.

**T9. Tiny faces and scale normalisation (face detection, COCO small objects): up-sample, do not shrink the stride.**
- Role: a 10-25 px face = a 5-20 px door or window symbol. Hu and Ramanan (CVPR 2017; PDF read, 2026-11-14): a
  template for 25x20 faces run on a 2x up-sampled image gains 69% -> 75% (their Fig. 7, "overall accuracy"), and for
  objects under 40 px height they recommend a 2x larger canonical resolution; the finest scale of their pyramid is a
  2x interpolated image (V, Sec. 3.2).
- Singh and Davis, SNIP (CVPR 2018; PDF read): on COCO R-FCN ResNet-50, small-object AP 16.3 at (800,1200) single
  scale, 19.5 with multi-scale test, 21.4 with SNIP (Table 2); on ImageNet low-resolution classification they find "it
  is better to up-sample images 2 times and then fine-tune the network pre-trained on high-resolution images" than to
  halve the first-layer stride (Sec. 3). Reason: the pretrained filters were learned on object sizes the up-sampling
  restores.
- What breaks: line art up-sampled with Lanczos has blurred 1-px strokes and the earlier report already says to keep
  generative super-resolution out of the count path; plain interpolation adds no strokes. The evidence is for COCO-like
  photos and faces, not drawings; a DETR pretrained on COCO has seen few 6-px objects. Nobody measured stride-4 level
  vs 2x input on a DETR head to head on drawings (see T1, T23, T31 for the nearest evidence).
- Usable as is? The idea only (no weights needed): resize each plan so the median symbol is at least 24-32 px.

**T10. Detector shoot-out on engineering drawings (AEC facade drawings, EC3 2026; large objects only).**
551 drawings at 300 DPI (3509x2480 up to 14042x9934 px), 9 layout classes (title block, tables, sections, elevations);
trained on a 16 GB T4 (V, PDF read via curl + pypdf, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18997, 2026-11-14). Test mAP50:95:
RF-DETR-M 0.890 (mAP50 0.949, 22 FPS at 1344x800), Faster R-CNN R50 0.779 (0.930), YOLOv12-M 0.755 (0.871, 640x640 input),
Qwen3-VL-8B 0.668, DocLayout-YOLO pretrained on DocLayNet 0.589 ("domain interference": document-layout weights made it
worse than COCO weights). Evidence for choosing a DETR over Faster R-CNN or a YOLO on drawings, and against document
pretraining. What breaks: objects are title blocks and sections, not 5-20 px symbols; the YOLO row has a smaller input
(640 vs 1344x800), so the comparison is confounded; no augmentation; one split. Belongs partly to L9 (sheet layout).
Code/data: not stated in the text read.

**T11. Symbols on electrical layout plans and construction drawings (floor-plan-adjacent; two papers).**
- SkeySpot (arXiv 2508.10449, Aug 2025; PDF read via curl + pypdf, 2026-11-14): 45 scanned UK electrical layout plans,
  2,450 instances, 34 classes (sockets, switches, lights, radiators), 13 test images with 598 instances. COCO-pretrained
  YOLOv8 (AGPL, out for us) mAP50 81.12 / mAP50-95 48.24 (5-fold mean) vs Faster R-CNN 48.62 / 26.13 after 4,000
  iterations (V, Table II). Per class (V, Table III): 97.0 AP50 for double sockets (348 train instances), 98.7 for
  downlighters (282), 91.96 for light switches (218), but 43.0 for USB double sockets (78 train, confused with double
  sockets) and 21-24 for classes with 2-3 train instances. Lesson for us: with 45 plans, classes with over about 100
  instances reach 92-99 AP50; look-alike classes and rare classes fail. No input size, no exact-count metric, no
  licence stated for the dataset in the text read. Augmentation used: flip, rotation, random crop, brightness.
- Construction diagrams for material takeoff (IJDAR 2024): on high-resolution industry drawings, a YOLO-based detector
  mAP 79% and a Faster R-CNN-based one 83% (R, abstract via OpenAlex; the publisher's page sits behind a JavaScript
  client challenge, which I did not try to bypass). The search snippet had these two reversed: use the abstract.
- What breaks: both are CAD-style symbols with a legend; CubiCasa symbols are drawn by many architects, with no legend
  to crop from. A GitHub project (Electrical-Symbol-Detector-for-Construction-Plans, YOLOv8, README read) trains on
  symbols cropped from the plan's own legend pasted on synthetic pages, with a review UI; it reports no numbers.

**T13. Counting only needs centres (YOLSO, Deep Watershed Detector, SKU-110K): an inference from three sources, mine.**
- Deep Watershed Detector (Tuggener et al., 2018, arXiv 1805.10548; PDF read via curl + pypdf, 2026-11-14): a fully
  convolutional net predicts an energy map with small conical peaks at object centres plus class and box maps; one cut
  of the energy map at a level gives the objects, so the number of objects per page does not matter "as long as their
  centers do not overlap". Trained on 960x960 random crops, batch size 1, inferred on whole pages. DeepScores AP@0.5
  for noteheadBlack 0.8619, but AP@0.25 0.9200 and the authors say box regression "is not very accurate" while staccato
  dots (tiny) are found well; overlapping noteheads on handwritten MUSCIMA++ are its weak spot (V, Tables 1-2).
  Code github.com/tuggeluk/DeepWatershedDetection MIT (gh api licence field; last push 2023-03, TF1 era), weights not
  checked. On DeepScoresV2 it was beaten by Faster R-CNN + HRNet except for the rarest classes (V, DSV2 Table IV).
- The three together: for an exact count the localisation tolerance is free, only the centre must be hit once; AP at
  IoU 0.5-0.95 rewards box precision we do not need (DWD AP rises 0.86 -> 0.92 when overlap needed drops from 0.5 to
  0.25). So rank candidate detectors by centre-match recall/precision at a distance tolerance and by per-image
  exact-count rate, not by AP50:95. No paper I read reports that metric; SKU-110K reports per-image count MAE only.
- What breaks: window strips and doors whose swing arcs make centres ambiguous (where is the centre of a door symbol:
  leaf or arc?); needs one consistent centre definition from the SVG boxes, and symbol centres that do not coincide
  (a window inside a door-like frame).

**T14. Sim-to-real on noisy scans (music object detection; belongs mainly to L8, results kept here because it is a detector measurement).**
- Role: clean rendered scores = clean rendered plans (ResPlan); scanned, stained, folded real pages = our scans and
  phone photos. RealScores test: 12 pages, 12,553 annotations, 61 classes; a detector trained on the clean set gets
  36.0% AP@0.25 on it (V, Table 3/4). The authors evaluate at overlap 0.25 "due to the very small object sizes" because
  usable detections often fall below 0.5 (V), the same point as T13.
- Second sim-to-real datum (PID2Graph Table A5, re-read 2026-11-14, V): Relationformer trained on synthetic P&IDs only scores symbol mAP 26.67 on the real OPEN100 sheets, against 73.49 with a synthetic+real mix; on synthetic test sheets 63.20 vs 78.62. Synthetic-only training does not transfer; keep real plans in the mix.
- What worked: ScoreAug (see Top 5): synthetic page over a real blank scan, 60% small-angle rotation, 50% flips,
  Gaussian blur 10%, brightness; "Finalise" = 200 more epochs on whole pages, because the crop-trained model "lacks
  global awareness (predicting noteheads at the corner of the page)" (V). Adversarial domain adaptation gave 48.9%.
  Snapshot ensembles (checkpoints along a cyclic cosine schedule) improved AP by up to 5.2 points and were used for
  confidence ratings (V, Table 5; for L10).
- What breaks: their images are 1-colour pages with a single paper texture; ours include colour, rotated sheets, and
  the training set is real already; the gain applies to the synthetic share. Multi-hour budgets: models trained 1,000-2,000
  epochs; far over our 3 h.

**T15. Query cap is a hard count ceiling per tile (RF-DETR repo, read 2026-11-14, V).**
- `src/rfdetr/config.py` on the default branch: detection Nano/Small/Medium/Large and Base `num_queries = num_select = 300`; Seg Nano and Small 100, Seg Medium and Large 200, Seg XL 300. A maintainer states in issue 780 that Seg Nano "has an upper bound of 100 objects" and advises XL at lower resolution for dense scenes (V, comment 2026-03-06). The reporter still missed cells with SAHI 384 px tiles at overlap 0.1-0.3 on 500-1500 objects per image, and maintainers have no dense segmentation benchmark ("no rf100vl for seg").
- The cap cannot be raised while keeping the published weights (V, `config.py` validator read 2026-11-14): `num_queries` and `group_detr` above the variant default are in a `breaking_on_increase` list because they "add slots whose shape differs", and the config warns that the checkpoint will not load cleanly; lowering them is fine. So for a tile with more objects than the cap the options are a larger variant (Seg-XL 300, detection 300), a smaller tile, or a re-initialised query set (E: retrained from the backbone, not a quick fine-tune).
- DSC itself ran RF-DETR-Seg-L with 768 x 768 inputs and 200 queries on drawings up to about 9624 x 2648 px (Europe PMC full text, re-read 2026-11-14, V), so their per-tile object count stayed under the cap; the paper does not report objects per tile.
- Consequence for Top 1: Seg-L at 200 queries per 768 px tile is fine if a tile holds fewer than about 100 symbols (E; DETR heads need slack); check the max symbols per tile on dev before choosing a tile size. A tile with more objects than queries loses objects without any error.
- Other issue facts (V, issue pages): 879 "minimum detectable object scale" has no measured bound, maintainer answer is to crop into patches and compare with RF100-VL object scales; 780 reports ONNX CUDA backend poor for transformers, TensorRT preferred, batched 4 slices + 1 full image gave only about 13% latency gain on XL-seg FP16 624 px (RTX 5070).
- RF-DETR dependencies (pyproject.toml, read 2026-11-14, V): core requires only requests, numpy, torch, torchvision, tqdm, pydantic, supervision (MIT), pyDeprecate (Apache-2.0); the train extra adds peft and ultrafast-pycocotools; no Ultralytics, no GPL or AGPL package in the core or train lists. Extras' licences (gh api, V): ultrafast-pycocotools BSD-2-Clause, peft Apache-2.0, albumentations (optional [augment] extra, <3.0) MIT on the repo I queried; check the exact installed package's licence file before shipping, since the albumentations project has changed hands and fork before (E, not checked).
- Pointers, not read, L1 scope: door-detection papers on floor plans with Co-DETR (arXiv 2509.17283, 2508.07714) and the AECV-Bench counts of doors/windows by multimodal LLMs (2601.04819); seen as search results only (R-).

**T16. Chemical-structure and molecule detection (new adjacent field; mainly a caution, V).**
- Role: atoms and bonds drawn as lines with small text labels = symbols touching lines on a plan. MolMiner treats atoms and bonds as YOLO detection classes (overall mAP@.5 97.5%, search listing, R-); MolDet is YOLO11 (AGPL family) for molecule regions in pages; MolMole's page is CC BY-NC-ND and MolDet-Bench CC-BY-NC-SA. None is usable as code or weights for us.
- The one transferable fact (V, arxiv.org/html/2512.15098v2, Uni-Parser, read 2026-11-14, line in Sec. on layout backbone): "RT-DETRv2, YOLOv12, and D-FINE can exhibit training instability in our layout detection task"; they picked a modified DETR instead and say DocLayout-YOLO's changes gave only marginal gains. One sentence, no numbers, a different task (page layout, 100+ MB data). Use as a prompt to log loss curves and run a second seed when fine-tuning D-FINE, not as evidence against it.

**T17. Class-agnostic symbol finder trained on synthetic shapes, electrical housing diagrams (Master's thesis, Chalmers 2026; read in full by curl + pypdf on 2026-11-14, V).**
- Role: their symbol = our window/door/fixture; a legend gives the reference, ours is a class list. Setup: 10,000 synthetic 1024x1024 images of random geometric shapes plus crossing wires (single class "symbol"), no real training images; test diagrams cut into 1024 px tiles with 20% overlap, global NMS; second pass on predicted boxes to split merged neighbours. Test set 7,799 annotated symbols, 98% of them under 4,000 px of median area.
- Result (V, Table 4.2): recall 0.57 (RT-DETRv2 R50, 20 epochs, Hugging Face) / 0.70-0.81 (YOLOv8s, YOLO11m, YOLO26m) / 0.62-0.88 (Faster R-CNN, 1024 px alone up to multi-scale with weighted box fusion); precision only 0.15-0.26 for all, so about 3-4 false boxes per true one. Synthetic-only training gives usable recall but not counts; consistent with PID2Graph Table A5 (T14).
- What breaks, size-wise (V, Table 4.4, recall by median symbol area): symbols of 0-1,000 px area: YOLO 0.825, Faster R-CNN 0.873, RT-DETR 0.095; 1,000-2,000: 0.902 / 0.970 / 0.622; 2,000-4,000: 0.897 / 0.977 / 0.588; 4,000-6,000: 0.404 / 0.575 / 0.757. The thesis's RT-DETRv2 (stride-32 top-level attention, one 1024 px pass) is the worst on the smallest symbols and the best on the largest: the same stride argument as Dome-DETR and RF-DETR's single P4 level (T1). A tile-size or up-sampling test must report recall by symbol area.
- Licence: thesis, no code. YOLO runs are Ultralytics (AGPL), not usable; the RT-DETRv2 checkpoint is Apache-2.0 in the original repo (E, not checked here).
- Multi-scale + box fusion raised recall (0.62 -> 0.88) but multiplied false boxes (16k -> 38k); fine for a proposal stage, bad for exact counts (V).

**T22. DQ-DETR (ECCV 2024, arXiv 2404.03507v5): a per-image count class sets the number of queries (read in full 2026-11-14 via pwfetch, V).**
- Role: the "number of instances per sheet varies from 1 to 2,267" problem of AI-TOD-V2 is our "rooms with 2 windows vs 40 windows" problem. A counting module (dilated-conv density map on the largest feature map) classifies each image into 4 count levels (cut points 10, 100, 500, chosen from the dataset's count statistics) and the level picks how many queries the decoder uses; the authors deliberately classify instead of regressing the count, because regressing a count over such a range "hurts the detection performance".
- Numbers (V, Table 2): AI-TOD-V2 test AP 30.2 with ResNet-50 vs 25.9 for the re-run DINO-DETR baseline (+4.3) and 25.7 for RFLA; small-object AP 15.3 vs 12.7. Built on DINO, 6+6 layers, ResNet-50.
- Licence: both repositories listed for the code (hoiliu-0801/DQ-DETR, Katie0723/DQ-DETR) have no licence (gh api, V): idea only, cannot ship; same status as SKU-110K code. Dome-DETR (T18) is the Apache re-implementation of this density-to-queries idea on D-FINE.
- Use for us (E): the count-level classifier is a cheap **second counter** signal per tile (4 buckets, or finer) that also tells whether the query cap of T15 is at risk; not needed in the detector itself.

**T23. Is a stride-4 level what helps? The one controlled datum (abstract only, preprint not peer reviewed; page read 2026-11-14).**
- SAF-RTDETR (Research Square rs-11390432, CC BY 4.0, VisDrone2019 protocol of 100 epochs, three seeds): 31.14 +- 0.51 AP50:95 vs 28.46 +- 0.73 for RT-DETR-R50, APS +2.77; AI-TOD-v2 +4.54, HIT-UAV +1.00, UAVDT -0.34; 288 GFLOPs. Its abstract states that "a plain stride-4 level adds only 0.63 AP, whereas selective enhancement accounts for most of the gain" (V as text of the abstract; table and full text not opened, R for the mechanism).
- Second source, full text read 2026-11-14 (arXiv 2605.25046, TinyFormer, May 2026, paper licence CC BY-NC-ND, built on DEIMv2/DINOv3 so its code is out for us): on COCO, a 3-scale ViT-based DETR baseline has AP 57.13 / APS 38.50; adding a stride-4 level made by bilinear up-sampling of the ViT features and a fusion module gives 57.32 / 39.08 (+0.19 AP, +0.58 APS), while routing raw early-stage spatial features in (their SSA adapter) gives 58.38 / 39.33 and both together 58.50 / 40.94 (V, Table 4); the authors conclude that stride-16 tokenisation "causes irreversible spatial loss that coarse-feature fusion alone cannot recover". VisDrone val at 640 px (V, Table 3): D-FINE-S 25.8 AP, D-FINE-M 29.1, D-FINE-L 30.3, DEIMv2-L 30.4, YOLOv11-M 25.9, their best L 32.2. Same message as SAF-RTDETR: a stride-4 level alone buys little; raw high-resolution detail bypassing the ViT does.
- Reading for us (E): simply adding a P2 level to a DETR is not enough; aligned high-resolution fusion is what pays, and it is costly: 288 GFLOPs here (about twice RT-DETR-R50's roughly 136, my recollection of the RT-DETR paper, not re-read), and in Dome-DETR's table the 4-level D-FINE-S* needs 112 GFLOPs at 800 px against 25 for stock S at 640 (V, see Top 3). For 12 GB and 3 hours, up-sampling the plan (T9) plus a patch/stride-matched model (T1) is cheaper than a custom four-scale head. Other 2025-26 four-scale DETR variants seen only as search listings (not opened): MS-RTDETR, RT-DETR++, FSDC-DETR (DEIMv2-L base, APS 24.4 -> 31.3, non-commercial base), HMF-DEIM (DEIM base, APS .135); all aerial, none with usable code known.

**T24. SAHI's defaults are a containment merge, and they merge boxes (obss/sahi source, read 2026-11-14 via gh api, MIT licence, V).**
- `get_sliced_prediction` defaults: `postprocess_type = "GREEDYNMM"`, `postprocess_match_metric = "IOS"` (intersection over the smaller area), `postprocess_match_threshold = 0.5`. A kept prediction absorbs every prediction that matches it, and `merge_object_prediction_pair` combines boxes, masks and scores into the kept one (docstring of `_apply_merge`). Options are NMM, GREEDYNMM, NMS and a Locality-Sensitive NMS marked experimental.
- Role: this is the cheap off-the-shelf form of the DSC containment idea (Top 1); independently confirmed by T30 (greedy merging and Weighted Boxes Fusion merge distinct neighbours in dense scenes; NMS with IoS was best on their calibration split). What breaks for counts (E, from the code's behaviour): **merging reduces the count** wherever two different true objects overlap by 50% of the smaller box (a window strip whose panes touch or a small symbol drawn inside a larger one); DSC suppresses (drops the contained candidate), SAHI default merges (grows the box). For exact counts use `postprocess_type = "NMS"` with `postprocess_match_metric = "IOS"`, or apply the merge only to pairs that cross a tile border (T3), and compare on dev with window strips.
- Also (V from the RF-DETR issue 780 thread): SAHI slices run one forward pass each; no batched export exists in RF-DETR, so tile count drives latency.

**T25. RF-DETR confidence flips with the ViT patch grid, a direct threat to exact counts (RF-DETR issue 1599, opened 2026-10-05, read with comments 2026-11-14 via gh api; the reproduction is a community comment, V for what it reports, not maintainer-confirmed).**
- Report: for the same 512 px tile shifted by whole pixels, a Seg model's box and mask IoU stay 0.90-0.95 while the class confidence goes 0.91 -> 0.49 (shift 11 px) -> 0.88 (shift 12 px), at worst 0.80 -> 0.003. A commenter reproduced it on the pretrained COCO checkpoints (Detection-Large: one person 0.71 -> 0.27 -> 0.69 over a 16-px shift, box IoU >= 0.94; worst case 0.71 -> 0.12) and found the period equals patch size x (tile size / model resolution): 8.00 px for Seg-2XL at 512 -> 768, 12.00 for 768 -> 768 with no resize, 16.00 for Large at 704 -> 704; encoder features are equivariant only to whole-patch shifts (cosine similarity 0.99 at 12 px but 0.89 at 6 px).
- Why it matters for us (E): a fixed confidence threshold turns this into missing or extra objects, so per-image counts change with the tile origin; it is the same effect DSC's RandomShift trains against (Top 1). Mitigations named in the thread: shift test-time augmentation at offsets {0, P/2}^2 (their offline simulation cut the median per-object confidence range from 0.118 to 0.032); a tile stride that is not a multiple of the patch period (for example 388 instead of 384 for 8-px periods) so overlapping tiles see different phases; sub-patch translation augmentation in training. None is measured on drawings.
- Training-cost datum, same repo (issue 1460, V100 16 GB, closed 2026-09, RF-DETR 1.10.1): Seg-Large at resolution 648, batch 2 with 8 accumulation steps, AMP fp16: 400 training batches took about 4 min 14 s per epoch and used about 4.9 GB peak memory, but validation ran out of memory in mask matching (no fix in the thread; the 1.11.0 release notes then list a change that converts masks to float 32 rows at a time instead of materialising every matched mask of a class, because a densely annotated class could OOM, V, so the problem is addressed in later versions). So a 3k-image fine-tune fits a 12 GB card for training (E: about 800 images per 4 minutes, roughly 5 minutes per 1,000 images), but validation of segmentation models was a memory risk before 1.11.0; a box-only Detection model avoids mask matching (E).
- Vendor documentation (RF-DETR docs, fine-tuning cookbook and training-parameters pages, read 2026-11-14 via pwfetch, R for the estimate, V for the table): "50 epochs on a ~1,500-image dataset takes roughly 10-25 minutes on a modern GPU (RTX 3090, A10, T4)" (an unmeasured vendor estimate, no model size or resolution given; it is much faster than the issue-1460 datum above, which was Seg-Large at 648 px, so the estimate fits small models at low resolution only, E); recommended batch settings for an effective batch of 16: RTX 3090/4090 24 GB batch 8 x 2 accumulation, T4 16 GB 4 x 4, RTX 3070 8 GB 2 x 8 (V), so an 8-12 GB card is possible with small micro-batches. **Resolution must be divisible by patch_size x num_windows (32 for RF-DETR-Small: patch 16, 2 windows)** (V, cookbook comment), which constrains custom values such as 1120 or 1344 (both multiples of 32; 1536 too) and ties to the unsupported-resolution fallback in issue 1001 (T27).
- Defaults (RF-DETR training-parameters page and cookbook, read 2026-11-14, V as documented): 100 epochs, lr 1e-4 and encoder lr 1.5e-4, step scheduler (cosine optional), early stopping off by default; `multi_scale=False` and `expanded_scales=False` switch off the multi-resolution training augmentation and "shorten epoch time by up to 40%", described as fine for most small-dataset fine-tunes (vendor claim, R). Scale jitter is a trade for us: it hurts nothing when symbols have a fixed pixel size, but plans vary 10x in scale, so test it on dev rather than assume (E).
- Version context (RF-DETR GitHub releases, read 2026-11-14 via gh api, V): the current release is 1.12.1 (2026-11-12); 1.10.0 (2026-09-04) states training steps about 23% faster at the default batch size and 1.25-2.26x DataLoader throughput on input-bound runs, 1.11.0 added opt-in CUDA-graph replay for training and dynamic-batch TensorRT engines (which answers the batched-slices request of issue 780 for TensorRT only), 1.12.0 added INT8 export. The hours data above (issue 1460 used 1.10.1) therefore predate the speed-ups and the cookbook estimate; pin one version and time one epoch before trusting any of them (E).

**T30. "When does sliced inference pay off?" (Fraunhofer IOSB, arXiv 2611.12657v3, 11 Nov 2026; read 2026-11-14 via pwfetch, abstract, Table 1 text and limitations V; CNN detectors, not DETRs).**
- Design: seven dense detectors from four families (YOLO variants, FCOS and others) on VisDrone and DOTA v1.5, hypotheses fixed before the test run, tuning on a calibration split only, one evaluation on held-out test data.
- Findings (V): (1) on VisDrone "a single pass trained at a higher resolution outperforms tiling with box fusion for every detector that could be trained at that resolution" (2048 px single pass vs 1280 px plus tiles: +2.8-3.5 AP on val, +1.8-3.1 on test-dev; standard SAHI the weakest setting); at equal compute a higher resolution helps more than a larger model. (2) Where the image is far beyond trainable size (DOTA), tiles beat the best single pass by 4.3-5.6 mAP50 at 2048 px. (3) Merge rule: their baseline merge is NMS with intersection over the smaller box, "the best box-level rule on calib"; greedy merging and Weighted Boxes Fusion "merge distinct neighbours in dense scenes" (V, text near Table 1). This is an independent confirmation of T24: SAHI's default GREEDYNMM lowers counts of touching neighbours; use NMS with IoS.
- Their new merge, SCAHI (Apache-2.0 per the paper), accumulates dense per-position outputs over all passes with equal coverage weights and reads objects off a peak grid fine enough to keep crowded neighbours apart; +1.4-2.0 AP over box fusion on identical passes; it needs dense per-position predictions (anchor-free CNN heads) and the authors state that query-based detectors such as RT-DETR need a sparse variant (V, limitations), so it does not drop into RF-DETR or D-FINE.
- Consequence for Top 1/Top 2 (E): for plans below the trainable resolution (RF-DETR-Large trained at 1344 px on a 22.5 GB card with gradient checkpointing in T28; nobody I read tried 12 GB, so the limit there is unmeasured) a single higher-resolution pass is the first thing to try; tiling stays for sheets beyond that (DSC's 9624 px) and then NMS with IoS, same tile geometry in training and inference. Corrects nothing in the ranking; it moves "single pass first, tiles second" up the run-first list (item 1 already says so).

**T31. Inference at a higher resolution than training, no retraining (arXiv 2608.03136, AI City Challenge 2026 Track 6, RF-DETR-Large with a DINOv2 ViT-S backbone; abstract and method read 2026-11-14 via pwfetch, V; exploratory, one submission per configuration, hidden aggregate AP).**
- A checkpoint trained at 704 x 704 evaluated frozen at 1120 x 1120 (2.53x the pixels) raised aggregate AP from 0.3272 to 0.3654 (+0.0382), "with the largest relative gain on small objects", without any parameter update; a warm-start fine-tune at 1120 px scored lower (0.3470) on the hidden set although its in-domain validation AP rose from 0.767 to 0.789, but the authors say the two used different confidence thresholds (0.05 vs 0.01), so it is descriptive only. In-domain validation was not a reliable selection signal under their cross-city shift; not our situation (our dev and eval images share the plan style), but it is a reason to keep a held-out split of unlike plans (E).
- Use for us (E): cheapest first experiment for the 5-20 px symbols: take the tiled or whole-plan RF-DETR fine-tune and evaluate it at 1.5-2.5x the training pixels on dev before spending a training run on resolution; confirm with exact-count rate (T13), since AP gains can hide count flips (T25). Not measured on drawings; one dataset.
- Related, abstract only (arXiv 2609.06581, 6 Sep 2026, abs page read 2026-11-14, R): a training-free method that applies counterfactual scale interventions to frozen detectors and uses decoder-internal evidence to pick the candidates, claiming AP and AP_small gains over 27 combinations of nine frozen detectors and three datasets; no code or licence seen, drawings not tested; it supports the idea that scale at inference is a lever on frozen weights.

**T32. SO-DETR, an Apache-2.0 small-object DETR with a short schedule (repo read 2026-11-14 via gh api; the paper itself not opened, so its numbers are R, quoted from TinyFormer's table).**
- Repo `Ultraman0729/SO-DETR`: Apache-2.0 (V), MMDetection 3.x project, config `so_detr-4scale_r50_8xb2-12e_coco` (config file read, V: `max_epochs = 12`, 2 images per GPU, base batch 16, `num_queries = 900`, ResNet-50 initialised from ImageNet, no COCO detection pretraining), needs the VMamba environment with compiled CUDA extensions (`mamba-ssm`), 0 stars, last push 2026-04-03, no pretrained weights listed in the README. Datasets named: SODA-D, AI-TOD, VisDrone.
- Numbers seen only through another paper (R): VisDrone val at 640 px, SO-DETR-R18 29.9 AP / 49.0 AP50 with 20.5 M parameters, SO-DETR-R50 31.5 / 51.5, against D-FINE-M 29.1 / 48.3 and D-FINE-X 31.6 / 51.5 in the same table (TinyFormer Table 3, V for the table, R for provenance).
- Reading (E): the only Apache-2.0 repo I found whose design target is small objects and which trains in 12 epochs, but it brings an exotic state-space encoder with CUDA compilation (fragile on WSL), one maintainer account and no weights; keep as a watch item, not a candidate.

**T33. RF-DETR, D-FINE and others are in Hugging Face transformers (repo tree and docs page read 2026-11-14 via gh api, V).**
- `src/transformers/models/` contains `rf_detr`, `d_fine`, `deimv2`, `lw_detr`, `rt_detr`, `rt_detr_v2`; the repository is Apache-2.0. The RF-DETR docs page says it was contributed to transformers on 2026-05-07, loads the original library's checkpoints and Roboflow-platform-trained checkpoints through a weight conversion mapping, and lists the DINOv2 backbone, group-wise one-to-many training, multi-level projector and a 3-layer decoder (V). So a second, library-independent inference path exists under one Apache-2.0 dependency; whether fine-tuning through the transformers Trainer is supported and matches the original recipe I did not check (E: use the original `rfdetr` package for training; use the transformers port for serving if the dependency list matters).
- Caution: a `deimv2` model class existing in transformers does not change the DEIMv2 weights licence (non-commercial, T-list above); check the checkpoint card, not the library licence.

**T34. Synthetic-only DETR on P&IDs collapses on real sheets (SBC ERAMIARS short paper "On P&ID Symbol Recognition using Transformers", PDF read in full 2026-11-14 via curl + pypdf, V; undated in the PDF text, cites 2025 works, so 2025-26).**
- Setup: DETR with ResNet-50, trained on 1,000 synthetic sheets (700 full sheets by a layout algorithm plus 300 symbol grids, 40 classes, uniform class frequency), 640 x 640 crops at 50% overlap, 300 epochs with flips, rotations, translations, scaling, blur, jitter, elastic transforms; **about 70 hours on one RTX 3060 per variant** (V). Tests: synthetic hold-out, IPD (2 internal sheets) and PID2Graph OPEN100 (12 real sheets).
- Result (V, Table 1, mAP 50:95 in %): COCO-initialised 90.5 synthetic / 33.8 IPD / 4.0 OPEN100; ImageNet backbone 52.4 / 25.1 / 1.9; backbone pretrained as a classifier on the 40 synthetic symbols 91.6 / 35.3 / 4.9. Domain pretraining adds about 1 point; the real-sheet number is a fraction of the synthetic one. Failure modes named by the authors: background elements, high proximity, overlap, low-resolution regions, similar components; "under-representation in the synthetic training data".
- Read with PID2Graph Table A5 (T14: synthetic-only 26.67 vs mixed 73.49 symbol mAP50 on OPEN100) and the Chalmers thesis (T17): three independent sources say a synthetic-only first stage is not a counter. Note the different metrics (mAP 50:95 here, mAP50 there). Also the first hours datum for a classic DETR (70 h per 300 epochs on a 12 GB-class card), which is why the DETR-family here (RF-DETR, D-FINE) matters: they converge in tens of epochs, not hundreds (E).

**T35. RF-DETR vs D-FINE on small objects, COCO val2017 (RF-DETR paper, ICLR 2026, arXiv 2511.09554v2, Table 2 re-read 2026-11-14 via pwfetch, V; authors' own measurements).**
- AP / AP_S / AP_L: Nano: RF-DETR 48.0 / 25.2 / 70.0 vs D-FINE 42.7 / 22.9 / 62.1; Small: 52.9 / 32.0 / 73.0 vs 50.6 / 32.6 / 66.6; Medium: 54.7 / 36.1 / 73.8 vs 55.0 / 37.6 / 71.7; RF-DETR 2XL 60.1 / 43.2 / 76.2. So at S and M, D-FINE has the higher or equal small-object AP and the higher overall AP at M; RF-DETR's lead is in large objects and in the Nano size. Parameters: RF-DETR 30.5-33.7 M against D-FINE 3.8-19.2 M, FLOPs 32-79 G against 7-57 G (V).
- Also in the paper (V): higher resolution "improves small object detection performance" and the positional embeddings are pre-allocated for the largest resolution and interpolated for smaller ones; the number of queries is a tunable knob and queries "can be dropped at test time without retraining by discarding the lowest-confidence queries" (so the cap can fall at test time, not rise; T15).
- Consequence (E): on pure small-object grounds the two Apache DETRs are a toss-up (matches T12's "within 2 AP_S"); RF-DETR's case rests on fine-tuning behaviour (RF100-VL, T12), a published drawing recipe (Top 1, T28) and fewer reported fine-tuning failures (T27), not on a small-object edge. If a D-FINE run is attempted, start from the COCO weights with the fixes of T27.

## 5. Open questions and tools

Open questions
1. Which input scale makes our symbols match the aerial regime that D-FINE handles (about 12 px at 800x800)? Test: up-sample each plan so its longest side is 1024-1536 px, train D-FINE-S, and compare exact-count rate with a 640-px run.
