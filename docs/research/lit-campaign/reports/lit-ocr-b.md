Earlier reports extended, not repeated: `stoa-extract.md` (RapidOCR/PP-OCR measured, closed-vocabulary decoding, 0/180 classifier),

over line work).

## 0. Bottom line

1. No field has a published number for our exact case (2-4 letter labels, 5-12 px high, on thin-line drawings). The nearest measured cases are
   licence plates at 12-20 px width and historical-map place names. Nobody has measured it on floor plans.
2. What the sideways fields agree on, with numbers: (a) fine-tune a small recogniser on rendered crops; on scene text the real-data share needed was about 1% of the real set
   (UnionST); (b) a lexicon on the output is worth about +9 to +11 points of end-to-end F on maps and scene text (PaLeTTe, TiCLS); (c) vote over several
   views of the same text (the plate competitions); (d) a recogniser trained on arbitrarily oriented text scores 80.7 mean word accuracy on rotated benchmarks, one trained on horizontal-only text scores 8.4
   (PARSeq appendix J, Table 18, same data otherwise). Checkpoints trained on upright text only collapse on 90 and 180 degree text.
3. The strongest map-text spotters (DeepSolo, DPText-DETR, PaLeTTe weights) are non-commercial. They are out for shipping. TESTR has an Apache-2.0 LICENSE
   but sits on the non-commercial AdelaiDet framework, so it needs a legal check. The clean path is detector + PARSeq / SVTRv2 / PP-OCRv6 recogniser (all Apache-2.0 code and weights where stated).
4. Super-resolution (SR) is the one point where fields disagree. The XLPSR plate entry says SR is the biggest single gain at 12-20 px plate width
   (+2.00 on a 0-10 score). The ICPR 2026 competition organisers say there was no dominant design and the third place used no SR. Our earlier rule
   (no generative SR in the count path) stands; SR on text crops only, behind a vocabulary gate, is a "maybe" to test.

## 3. Transfer notes (what plays our object, why it should transfer, what breaks, usable as is?)

### 3.1 Historical maps (ICDAR MapText 2024/2025, mapKurator, PaLeTTe, TiCLS)
- **Plays the role of:** a room label ("MH", "K", "WC") is a place name on a map: short text at any angle, over line work, with a known vocabulary.
- **Measured there (V):** on MapText 2025 the best Task 3 word spotting (Rumsey) is 91.1 H-mean with 94.0 character accuracy; detection is "strong",
  recognition and linking are the hard parts. DeepSolo-style DETR spotters took the top ranks. About 25% of text instances sit between 30 and 90 degrees,
  and at 60-90 degrees the best spotter still recalls only 55.22% end to end on Rumsey-309 (47.17 on Grinnell-UMass-31; PaLeTTe Table 3, V). In the same table the permissive TESTR is close behind (53.38 and 46.18), DeepSolo is lower (44.84 and 36.14). Polygon-point boundaries beat Bezier boundaries on steep text.
- **Lexicon:** "none" vs "full lexicon" end-to-end F is 70.27 vs 79.13 on one set and 69.82 vs 78.75 on the other. Caution: the "full" lexicon is every
  word of the test set (S via fetch, consistent with the paper's table; the definition sentence I did not re-read), so it is an optimistic bound. Ours would be about 25-30 fixed Finnish codes, which is a much smaller lexicon than a test set of place names, so the gain should be at least as large.
- **Synthetic data:** SynthMap+ renders OSM names over background patches cut from real maps, lifting E2E none 55.9 to 69.8 (V Table 4) and
  SynthText + SynthMap+ alone gives 64.6. The transferable idea is the background: cut text-free patches (walls, hatching, furniture) from real plans and render labels on them.
- **The pipeline that already runs this at scale (V, demo paper arXiv 2306.17059 and repo README):** cut the map into 1000x1000 tiles, spot text with a TESTR-based Deformable-DETR model, merge tiles, then a post-OCR step compares each string with a vocabulary by edit distance and breaks ties by entity frequency; it has processed over 60,000 maps. The demo paper gives no accuracy figure for the post-OCR step. The repo has no LICENSE file and its derived output dataset is CC BY-NC 2.0 (README).
- **Same table, permissive model (V):** TESTR (Apache-2.0) trained on SynthText + human scene sets gets E2E 53.0 on Rumsey-309; adding SynthMap+ gives 65.9 (+12.9); SynthText + SynthMap+ without any human-annotated set gives 61.0. So the rendered-in-context data helps the permissive model by about as much as the non-commercial one (+13.9).
- **What breaks on our images:** (1) text height is not reported in any map paper I read, patches are 1000x1000 and the maps are scanned at high resolution; our labels are 5-12 px on 855 px images.
  (2) The strongest code is non-commercial: DeepSolo's LICENSE file carries the AdelaiDet "non-commercial" header; the PaLeTTe model licence is modified PolyForm Noncommercial. (3) Training needs 4 A100 40 GB for days (R); we have 12-16 GB for hours.
  (4) Labels on maps carry a language prior (place names); two-letter codes carry none, which is what TiCLS's character-level language model would not help.
  (5) Rotation on maps is any angle; ours is exact multiples of 90, which makes cheap voting possible (note 3.2).
- **Usable as is?** No. The ideas (background render, lexicon on output) transfer; the weights do not. TESTR (Apache-2.0) is the permissive spotter, fine-tuning needed.

- **Page orientation, for the sheet worker (L9):** PaddleOCR's `PP-LCNet_x1_0_doc_ori` is a 4-class (0/90/180/270) page classifier, 99.06% top-1 on an unnamed set, 7 MB (V, module page; no licence statement, no input size). Whether it works on plans with almost no text is not shown anywhere I read.

### 3.2 Scene text recognition (PARSeq, SVTRv2, CLIP4STR, RISTER, UnionST)
- **Plays the role of:** the recogniser for a cropped label.
- **Rotation (V):** PARSeq appendix J Table 17: attention models keep 70-86% mean word accuracy (ABINet 69.6, TRBA 82.3, ViTSTR 82.4, PARSeq_A 86.2) on 90/180/270 rotations of the upright benchmarks; CRNN (CTC) keeps 9.6%. Table 18: trained on horizontal-only text, PARSeq_A drops to 8.4 on rotated sets; trained on arbitrary-oriented text it gets 80.7 and its upright accuracy goes up (90.6 to 91.3). So the cheap fix is rot90 augmentation in fine-tuning, not an orientation classifier.
  RISTER (Aug 2026) gets 96.0 on Union14M Multi-Oriented without any orientation step, but no code is published. Its Table 5 also shows 128x128 input lifts SVTRv2 Multi-Oriented 89.5 to 90.2.
- **Synthetic data (V):** UnionST: synthetic-only is within 0.3 of real on Multi-Oriented (94.45 vs 94.74) and ahead on Multi-Words (87.26 vs 86.29); with 1% real (32k crops) the average matches full real. This is the best evidence for our "render crops overnight" plan. Synthetic is still 10.6 behind on "General" (74.87 vs 85.47), so real crops from our dev images for the last mile are needed.
- **Which randomisations mattered (UnionST ablation, 0.5M samples, flattened table, so the row/column mapping is my reading; V text):** adding global rotation sampled uniformly over 0-2 pi moved Union14M Multi-Oriented from 33.31 to 87.29 (the average from 47.35 to 63.82); the extra corpus types (random strings, one character removed) lifted the average to 70.60 (random-string "Contextless" 59.69 to 70.73, the closest analogue of our codes); fonts alone slightly lowered it (69.30) at this size; downsampling + transmission-distortion augmentation raised the average to 72.06 and the "General" set 53.69 to 65.27. Removing rotation from the full recipe cost about 15 average points (72.06 to 56.82). Fonts: 113,788 files collected from sources marked free for commercial use (V, their statement).
- **A closed list of about 37 entries, measured (V, arXiv 2111.06738, Microsoft, ar5iv page):** biasing the decoder of a line recogniser toward a 37-name list cut the error rate on name fields from 36.0 to 15.7 (-56%) on IAM; passport MRZ character error 8.5 to 5.4; driver-licence fields word error 5.7 to 3.7. Method: regular expressions compiled to a weighted finite-state transducer, one strength parameter, about 1 ms per line. It is soft biasing, not forcing, and text outside the list is barely hurt at moderate strength (about 0% change at the milder settings, +19% at the strongest). The Google custom-vocabulary CTC beam search (ICDAR 2023) says "substantial reduction in word error rate" but I only reached the abstract. This is the closest number to our "25-30 codes" case.
- **What breaks on our images:** (1) every benchmark here uses 94 or 36 characters and word-length text (average 5 or more); ours is 1-4 letters from a closed set, where a language model has nothing to work with (SVTRv2's guidance module is training-only and uses neighbouring characters, V). (2) The input is 32 px high; our crop is 5-12 px high and gets 3-6x upscaling before the net; no paper measured recognition at that scale on line-art background. (3) Dark clutter (walls, hatch, furniture lines) touches the characters; scene-text sets have photographic backgrounds.
  (4) The public weights are trained on photographs, not drawings (leakage: no). (5) PARSeq's stock head has a 94-character charset; Finnish labels with ä, ö, å need a new charset configuration and a re-initialised head (maintainer reply, repo issue 9, V), whereas PP-OCRv6 lists Finnish and Swedish natively (tech report, V). (6) A 1-letter label ("K", "S") has too few strokes for any recogniser to separate from a furniture symbol, which is why plate/vocabulary voting matters more than architecture choice.
- **CTC or attention for very short strings (V, SMTR paper, arXiv 2407.12317 v3, AAAI 2025, Figure 1 caption):** "Attention-based methods like NRTR and PARSeq perform well in short text recognition, while the CTC-based FocalSVTR and the length-insensitive LISTER outperform them in long text but worse in short text." The figure is a plot; I found no per-length table. It points to an attention decoder (PARSeq, or SVTRv2-AR which the LRLPR third place and UnionST used) for 1-4 character labels, and away from a pure CTC head (SVTRv2 CTC, PP-OCRv6's inference head). The XLPSR entry also saw a CTC member hurt a vote (plates; see the lexical-snap note below for the opposite finding on rare strings). Counter-evidence (V): the LRLPR second place used a CTC head (SE-ResNet + Transformer encoder + STN) with layout constraints and reached 81.73% vs 82.13% for the winner; those are fixed 7-character strings, not 1-4 character variable ones. So CTC vs attention for our labels is open: test both on the same crops. Not measured on our labels.
- **Usable as is?** Fine-tuning needed. All three Apache-2.0 code bases fine-tune on one 12 GB GPU (R; I did not run them).

- **Lexical snap in attention decoders (V full text, arXiv 2609.00816, Sep 2026):** stratifying the six standard benchmarks by word rarity x character-trigram novelty, accuracy at the rare/rare corner is 10-18 points below the centre across nine English recognisers; scaling CLIP4STR-Base 158M to Huge 1.0B leaves the corner unchanged (86.9 to 86.5). The cause is localised to the autoregressive decoder's language prior, which snaps unfamiliar strings to a familiar word. 16 inference/loss/data/adapter fixes (including beam and LM rescoring) did not improve the corner and aggregate together; only a confidence-routed AR + CTC ensemble helped. **What it means for us:** room codes are exactly rare short strings, so an AR recogniser (PARSeq, CLIP4STR) may output a plausible wrong word; a closed-vocabulary check plus abstention (top 5 item 2) should therefore compare against the raw output, and a CTC model (SVTRv2) is worth keeping as a cross-check, which differs from the plate result where a CTC third model hurt the vote (different setting: plates, score rule +2/-1). Test both on our crops; no code or weights licence read.

### 3.3 Licence plates (XLPSR entry, ICPR 2026 LRLPR, LP-LLM, 2026)
- **Plays the role of:** a short closed-alphabet string, 7 characters, plates 12-20 px wide with characters 2-3 px of stroke, motion blur and compression. This is the closest published case to our labels in pixel terms.
- **Measured (V):** XLPSR: PARSeq+CLIP4STR no SR 7.27, PARSeq + SR 9.27, two models + SR + 2:1 vote 9.62, + abstention below 0.33 9.73 (max 10). The scoring rule paid +2 correct, -1 wrong, 0 abstain, so abstaining under one third confidence breaks even. Hard format rules inside the vote cost -0.11 (they removed correct edge cases); a third model with a different decoder family (CTC) made the vote worse.
  LRLPR: 82.13% exact for the winner (joint SR + OCR teacher-student, HATFIR/MambaIRv2); third place used SVTRv2-AR with no SR at 80.17%; "no single dominant design"; the shared trait was fusing the five frames. The organisers add that confidence quality (confidence gap) matters for manual verification.
- **What breaks on our images:** (1) both use 5-10 video frames of the same plate and fuse them; we have one image, so the substitute is several derived views (four rotations, two or three scales). (2) The plate has a fixed layout; floor-plan labels are free-floating. (3) The SR is a GAN trained on natural images; neither paper measures invented characters, and a wrong letter in a 2-letter code (MH vs NH, K vs R) is exactly the error our counts cannot absorb. (4) XLPSR is a 1-run, 39-sequence validation result by an independent entrant; read it as a design hint, not a proof.
- **A measured hallucination case from the SR side (V):** GLYPH-SR (arXiv 2510.26339, Oct 2025) reports that on SVT x4 the diffusion SR DiffBIR scores 47.82 MANIQA / 71.18 MUSIQ yet "suffers from text hallucination", giving OpenOCR F1 of 38.73, while StableSR keeps text F1 high by restoring conservatively and scores poorly on perceptual metrics. So perceptual quality and legible text trade off in diffusion SR; GAN SR (Real-ESRGAN, the HAT family) was not singled out as hallucinating there. The paper's own method needs a VLM + latent diffusion model, so it is not a candidate for us.
- **Usable as is?** HAT code Apache-2.0, weights licence unstated. Idea transfers (vote + abstain); SR must be A/B tested against Lanczos with exact-match on our crops.

### 3.4 Engineering drawings and P&ID
- **Plays the role of:** tags, line numbers and dimensions over line work, rotated text, tiled inference.
- **Found (V):** the PCB engineering-drawing VLM paper reproduces eDOCr2 and a YOLO-OBB + Florence-2 pipeline on its 26-drawing validation split: eDOCr2 localisation F1 0.21, content exact match 0 of 78 content references (20 drawings); boxes are axis-aligned and there is no rotation step. The Chalmers/McDermott line-number detector (ICMLA 2024) reports recall above 90% where earlier detectors "limit" capture of whole line numbers, but I could only read its abstract (IEEE page returned an empty body), so the method is not assessed. A P&ID tiling paper (IAAI 2025) appears only in a search snippet (S).
- **Found, with a usable number (V, full page via headless browser; CC BY paper):** a 2026 Frontiers in Built Environment paper on rebar annotations (1,005 drawings, 3000x4000 px and larger, 7,108 annotations, 14.6% vertical) trains recognisers on **5,054 crops** and reports word accuracy: CRNN 74.7, NRTR 83.2, ABINet 90.8, TrOCR-base 91.2, PARSeq 92.4, SVTR 94.6, their Transformer + local attention 96.3 (15.56M params, 18 ms per crop). Vertical text is rotated 90 degrees clockwise before reading; augmentation includes random rotation of +-5 degrees. Text height is not reported; code "will be released", dataset on request only (V, data statement). Takeaway: about 5k in-domain crops were enough to put PARSeq/SVTR above 92% on drawing annotations, and the older CTC-CRNN is 20 points behind.
- **What breaks:** no permissive code or weights, the data are private, the characters are digits and symbols on 3000+ px scans, not 5-12 px letters on 855 px images. Nothing here beats the scene-text route except the crop-count evidence above.
- **Usable as is?** No. **Skip.**

- **eDOCr, Frontiers in Manufacturing Technology 2023, doi 10.3389/fmtec.2023.1154132 (V full page, 2026-11-14):** the one engineering-drawing system I found that trains its recognisers on auto-generated synthetic samples only, with a **restricted alphabet per recogniser**: dimension recogniser (digits, a few letters, symbols) 10,000 samples, 3 epochs after an overfit at 5,000; information-block recogniser 50,000 samples, about 1 h 40 min; all on a 2 GB RTX 2060, starting from the keras-ocr CRNN with its top layer removed. On real drawings: detection precision and recall about 90%, recognition F1 94%, character error rate 8% (R, the authors' abstract; training loss did not converge, they say real-data fine-tuning would help). Code MIT (`gh api`, last push 2025-01). **What it means for us:** synthetic-only training of a small, closed-alphabet recogniser is cheap (hours, 2 GB GPU) and reached the nineties on drawings; its follow-up eDOCr2 scored 0 exact match on a different domain (PCB pages, see above), so the recogniser does not transfer without retraining on the target look. **What breaks:** CRNN from 2019 (keras-ocr), dimension strings not room codes, no rotation recogniser beyond its own orientation heuristics.

- **Schlagenhauf et al., Procedia CIRP (CIRP ICME 2022; arXiv 2205.02659; V full PDF, 2026-11-14), open, code on GitHub (licence not read):** a detector trained only on **generated technical drawings** (domain-knowledge generator) versus the same detector trained on 9 real drawings versus out-of-the-box Keras-OCR, tested on 171 text objects in real drawings. Detection rate 81.87% (generated, 7,000 images) vs 4.67% (9 real) vs 77.19% (Keras-OCR); correct recognition (1 - CER) 79.33% vs 47.91% (from only 8 detections, unreliable) vs 46.75%. Detection quality rose with the number of generated drawings (2,000, 5,000, 7,000). The authors report clear failures on **vertical text near other drawing elements**. This is the nearest published support for rendering training drawings (item 1) and for the rotated-text-over-lines difficulty. **What breaks:** dimension digits, small test set (171), 2022 models, no letters or closed vocabulary.

### 3.5 Text read by a small VLM on a rectified crop (ArabGeoText, edge-OCR survey)
- **Plays the role of:** the crop reader.
- **Measured (V):** ArabGeoText: detector mask -> oriented rectangle -> rectify -> bicubic upscale to at least 48 px height -> Qwen3.5-4B + LoRA reader: exact 37.2 for the direct VLM, 69.9 for the tool pipeline zero-shot, 84.3 after adapting on 20.5k crops (CER 4.6). Edge survey (ICCVW 2025, V): on cropped SVT, PaddleOCRv4 recogniser 86.84 vs Qwen2.5-VL-3B 92.89; on IC15 76.83 vs 80.31; the paper tests neither rotation nor size.
- **What breaks:** Arabic place names, not 2-letter codes; a 3-4B VLM is far above the 12 GB fine-tune comfort for a count pipeline; VLM hallucination on short strings is not measured by either paper. L7 (VLM) covers this: see that scope.

- **Where small text fails, measured (V full text, Hawkeye / BSTRB, arXiv 2610.41254, Oct 2026, first found as a paper citing UnionST):** on real blurry video frames VLM accuracy "begins to degrade at around 20 pixels of mean text height"; on clean generated digital text (Snellen-style rendered pages) the same models "hold accuracy down to roughly 5 pixels". Qwen3-VL 4B/8B fine-tuned with SFT then GRPO, every image upscaled 3x bicubic at train and test time, matches much larger VLMs overall and beats them on text at or below 20 px; the base models are run without upscaling. **For us:** our 5-12 px labels are rendered, not blurred, which is the regime the clean-text control says is still readable; the failure is blur and noise, not size alone. It also supports a 3x bicubic step before the reader. **What breaks:** food packaging and household objects in egocentric video, free text not codes, 4-8B VLMs (not for a 12 GB count pipeline), no code link or licence found in the text I searched.

### 3.7 Industrial closed-alphabet codes: tyre sidewall reader (Kazmi et al., IEEE T-ITS, accepted 2020; V full PDF, 2026-11-14)
- **What plays our object:** a 32-character code (digits and capitals) embossed on a low-contrast rubber surface, found by a HOG-CNN localiser, unwarped from a circle to a strip, read by a character CNN cascade.
- **Measured:** character classifier trained only on about 700K **synthetic** character images (several fonts, smear and shift to imitate emboss) reached under 5% training error; on real drive-by video the mean accuracy over six sample sets was 86% (73-95% depending on shadow, wear and texture). Typical errors are look-alike pairs in the closed alphabet (0 and 8 read as D and 3, J as I) and ghost detections on background.
- **What breaks on our images:** the code is found by an anchor ("DOT"); we have no anchor. Characters are read one by one, so letter-pair context (2-4 letter room codes) is lost. Old CNN, no code or weights released that I found; it is evidence only that synthetic-only training of a closed alphabet works at about 86% in a hard setting, and that look-alike pairs (0/O, I/1, 8/B) need a confusion-aware vocabulary check. Not ranked; supports top 5 items 1 and 2.

### 3.8 PP-OCRv6 recogniser size (not an adjacent field: a size note for the engine already in use)
- PP-OCRv6 recognition on the English and Latin-script benchmarks (tech report 2606.13108 Table 11, V full PDF text; the benchmark itself is Baidu's, size and content not stated in what I read): medium 88.4 / 88.0, small 86.3 / 84.0, tiny 77.3 / 63.8 (English / Latin). So **small** is the smallest size that holds up on Latin text; tiny drops 20 points on Latin. ### 3.9 Historical astronomical diagrams: text region detection (Baltaci et al., arXiv 2606.15886, ICDAR 2026; V full text via pwfetch, 2026-11-14)
- **What plays our object:** short labels (single symbols to paragraphs, median 8 regions per diagram) in any orientation over line drawings; 948 diagrams, 10,940 oriented text regions (CC BY 4.0 paper; dataset and code stated public, licence not read).
- **Measured (class-agnostic region F1, Table 3):** TESTR zero-shot from scene text 30.6; fine-tuned on the diagrams 80.4; fine-tuned without scene-text pretraining 47.4. A DINO-DETR polygon baseline (Poly-DETR) pretrained on **synthetic diagrams** reaches 79.7 after fine-tuning (73.2 without pretraining); DeepSolo++ fine-tuned 80.0 but 0.0 without pretraining. Six annotators re-annotating 20 diagrams agree near 100% below IoU 0.3, which the authors read as annotators agreeing on what a text region is.
- **What it means for us (my reading):** a detector needs in-domain fine-tuning (30.6 to 80.4) and synthetic pretraining on rendered diagrams is nearly as good as scene-text pretraining (79.7 vs 80.4). This backs top 5 item 1 for a detector as well as a recogniser, and TESTR (item 5) is the strongest permissive baseline on this drawing-like data, still under the licence question. **What breaks:** they detect regions only, no recognition; scripts are historical, not Latin room codes; more than 900 annotated diagrams, we have far fewer.

### 3.10 P&ID pipeline numbers: recogniser + format-aware correction rules (Shteriyanov et al., Computers and Chemical Engineering 204 (2026) 109436, published Oct 2025; V full text, open copy from the university repository)
- **What plays our object:** a tag with a fixed format over line work, found by a high-recall detector, read by a fine-tuned recogniser, then checked and corrected by deterministic rules built from a format template (components with meanings: size, fluid code, class). Four industrial projects, each with its own format.
- **Measured:** end-to-end accuracy 91.1% overall (83.2 / 97.8 / 91.3 / 88.2 per project); recognition 95.7% with a perfect detector. EasyOCR's CRNN did worst (omitted hyphens; 8 for B, 0 for D, O for 0, I for 1, G for 6); Tesseract made the same kinds of mistakes less often; their own recogniser beat both even without rules. Rules matter most when the raw reader makes a systematic slip: on one project the no-rules reader fell to 40% (reading "-" as ">" or dropping it) and the rules recovered it. Rules were written from an error analysis on a development set, confirmed safe by domain experts, and are **not** applied to components that mix letters and digits (a "0" read as "Q" stayed wrong): the remaining 4.3% of recognition errors are exactly those.
- **What it means for us:** the closest published analogue to "recogniser + closed vocabulary check": a confusion table from our own dev errors (0/O, I/1, 8/B, 5/S) plus format rules, applied only where the correction is unambiguous, and abstaining elsewhere (top 5 item 2). The rules were tuned per project, which matches our plan of tuning on the dev split. **What breaks:** private data, no code or weights, the recogniser's backbone is not stated in the text I read, tags are long (several components), ours are 1-4 letters from a list.

## 5. Expected accuracy on 2-4 character labels at 10 px, and the cheapest fix
- **Published:** none. The nearest: plates 12-20 px wide, 7 characters, 9.73/10 on a score where wrong costs -1 and abstain 0 (V); winner 82.13% exact on low-resolution plates (V). Map E2E 70-79 F on place names (V). None of these are our case.
- **Ours, measured earlier (earlier reports):** RapidOCR raw 23-81% exact per room type on 40 images; 2-letter codes at 5-10 px are the misses.
- **Cheapest fix, ranked by evidence per hour (my ranking, Est.):**
  1. Doubling the recogniser input height (PP-OCRv6 historical: -8.3 to -11.5 CER points; our own upscaling test was mixed per room type) and fine-tuning on rendered + real dev crops: 2-4 h to a first number.
  2. Vocabulary scoring with abstention: under 1 h to code, no training.
  3. Four-rotation voting: 1 h, costs 4x recogniser time on crops (cheap: crops are small).
  4. SR on crops: 1 h to test, not shipped unless it beats Lanczos on exact match.
- Finnish abbreviation list with sources: not in this angle's reach, nothing sourced found; left to the field and practical-fit workers.

### 5b. First test, in order (Est.: about half a day)
1. Cut 50-100 labelled crops (open question 1 decides how); keep 20% unseen.
2. Read them zero-shot with PP-OCRv6-small, PARSeq-S and SVTRv2-S at 3x and 6x bicubic upscaling; record exact match per recogniser and per upscale.
3. Add the four-rotation vote (0/90/180/270, keep the highest-confidence reading) and the closed-vocabulary gate; compare gate output with the raw output and keep a CTC model as a cross-check (see the lexical snap note in 3.2).
4. Only if the best exact match is still low: render synthetic crops (SynthTIGER, MIT) from our own vocabulary over real background patches and fine-tune the best recogniser (top 5 item 1). SR on crops (item 4) is tested last.

- AECV-Bench (arXiv 2601.04819, Jan 2026; abstract and keyword search of the PDF text): large multimodal models on floor plans reach up to 0.95 accuracy on OCR-style questions but 0.40-0.55 on door/window counting; it evaluates big VLMs, not small recognisers (L7 scope). Not ranked.
